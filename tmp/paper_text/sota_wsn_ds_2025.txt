                  Md. Alamin Talukder1, Majdi Khalid2 & Nasrin Sultana3

                    Intrusion detection systems are essential for securing wireless sensor networks (WSNs) and Internet of
                    Things (IoT) environments against various threats. This study presents a novel hybrid machine learning
                    (ML) model that integrates KMeans-SMOTE (KMS) for data balancing and principal component analysis
                    (PCA) for dimensionality reduction, evaluated using the WSN-DS and TON-IoT datasets. The model
                    employs classifiers such as Decision Tree Classifier, Random Forest Classifier (RFC), and gradient
                    boosting techniques like XGBoost (XGBC) to enhance detection accuracy and efficiency. The proposed
                    hybrid (KMS + PCA + RFC) approach achieves remarkable performance, with an accuracy of 99.94% and
                    an f1-score of 99.94% on the WSN-DS dataset. For the TON-IoT dataset, it achieves 99.97% accuracy
                    and an f1-score of 99.97%, outperforming traditional SMOTE TomekLink and Generative Adversarial
                    Network-based data balancing techniques. This hybrid approach addresses class imbalance and high-
                    dimensionality challenges, providing scalable and robust intrusion detection. Complexity analysis
                    reveals that the proposed model reduces training and prediction times, making it suitable for real-time
                    applications.

                  Keywords Intrusion detection systems, Wireless sensor networks, Internet of Things, Hybrid machine
                    learning, Model, Dimensionality reduction, Data balancing techniques

                    Internet of Things (IoT) networks and wireless sensor networks (WSNs) have become pivotal in modern
                    technology, playing a crucial role in numerous fields, including industrial automation, smart cities, agriculture
                    and healthcare1. WSNs consist of spatially distributed autonomous sensors that monitor and record
                    environmental conditions, while IoT networks enable interconnected devices to communicate and exchange
                    data seamlessly2. Despite their potential, these networks are vulnerable to numerous security threats, including
                    data tampering, denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, routing attacks, and eavesdropping, which can severely
                    compromise their performance and reliability3,4. The dynamic and resource-constrained nature of WSNs and
                    IoT networks presents unique security challenges5. Traditional methods for intrusion detection often fall short
                    due to their inability to adapt to the heterogeneous and evolving attack patterns in these networks6,7. Over the
                    years, several machine learning (ML)-based approaches have been proposed for intrusion detection systems
                    (IDS) in WSNs and IoT networks8,9. These approaches leverage the capability of ML to learn complex patterns,
                    enabling automated detection of anomalies and malicious activities with improved accuracy compared to rule-
                    based systems10,11.

                         ML has emerged as a powerful tool in IDS due to its ability to automatically identify complex patterns and
                    anomalies in network data12,13. ML models can effectively detect known and unknown attacks by learning from
                    historical data, offering dynamic and adaptive solutions14­17. Techniques like Decision Trees (DT), Random
                    Forests (RF), and Gradient Boosting classifiers provide high accuracy, making them ideal for real-time threat
                    detection in WSN and IoT environments18.

    Existing works in the wireless sensor and IoT network domain have demonstrated promising results using
various ML models such as DT, usfAD, one class classifier (OCC) and Gradient Boosting models19­22 to build
the IDS model. However, these methods often suffer from limitations, including imbalanced datasets and high
dimensionality of features, which degrade model performance. For instance, imbalanced datasets lead to biased
predictions favoring majority classes, while high-dimensional data increases computational costs and risks
overfitting.

    To address these limitations, we propose a novel hybrid ML model that integrates KMeans-SMOTE (KMS)
for data balancing with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction, and Random Forest
Classifier (RFC) as the core attack detection model. The proposed model mitigates the issues of data imbalance
and feature redundancy while maintaining high accuracy and efficiency. By employing KMS, the proposed
approach effectively balances minority and majority classes, ensuring equitable representation in the dataset.
PCA reduces the dimensionality of data, capturing essential features while eliminating noise, thereby enhancing
computational efficiency. Finally, the use of RFC ensures robust and interpretable decision-making.

    Our proposed methodology surpasses the limitations of existing techniques by offering a more balanced,
efficient, and scalable solution for intrusion detection in WSN and IoT networks. Through extensive
experimentation on WSN-DS and TON-IoT datasets, our model demonstrates superior performance in terms
of accuracy, precision, recall, and f1-score, thereby providing a robust framework for securing these critical
networks against evolving threats.

Objective
The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust hybrid ML model for intrusion detection in WSNs.
By integrating advanced data balancing techniques such as STL, GANs, and KMS, and reducing the curse of
dimensionality using PCA, the model aims to address the challenges of class imbalance and high-dimensional
data. This approach enhances detection accuracy, minimizes false positives, and ensures computational efficiency,
making it suitable for real-time applications in WSNs security.

Related works

IDS using ML have gained significant attention for securing WSNs. Various ML models have been applied to
IDS for detecting abnormal activities. These models effectively classify network traffic and identify intrusions,
though challenges such as class imbalance and high-dimensional data often hinder their performance. Recent
advancements incorporate hybrid techniques, ensemble learning, and deep learning (DL) to improve detection
accuracy and robustness.

Related works on WSN-DS
Talukder et al.23 introduced an IDS method that combined ML with the STL algorithm. This approach balanced
the dataset by generating synthetic minority instances and removing Tomek links, leading to improved detection
accuracy in WSNs. To ensure consistent and scalable input features, feature scaling through standardization was
applied. The STL resampling technique effectively addressed imbalanced datasets, reducing the risks of overfitting
and underfitting. An extensive evaluation using the WSN-DS dataset revealed the RFC model, achieving 99.92%
accuracy, precision, recall, and f1-score, in multiclass scenarios, demonstrating its effectiveness in identifying
and preventing intrusions.

    Karthikeyan et al.20 made significant advancements in IoT and WSN security by combining ML with the
Firefly Algorithm (FA). This work has two key contributions: first, the FA-ML technique enhances intrusion
detection accuracy in WSN-IoT systems, and second, it introduces a novel security optimization approach by
integrating ML with the Firefly Algorithm. The FA-ML method, utilizing an SVM model and optimized by the
Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO), achieved an impressive performance with 99.34% accuracy, 98.36% recall and
96.67% f1-score. These results demonstrate the FA-ML method's effectiveness as a robust security solution for
WSN-IoT systems.

Methodology

This study introduces a comprehensive methodology designed to enhance the intrusion detection performance
of ML models on the WSN and TON-IoT Network datasets. The methodology integrates advanced data
preprocessing, dimensionality reduction, and ML approaches, ensuring optimal accuracy and computational
efficiency. The steps involved are as follows:

· Data Collection: The study utilizes two benchmark datasets, WSN-DS and TON-IoT Network, to evaluate
   the proposed hybrid machine learning model for intrusion detection. These datasets contain diverse traffic
   patterns and attack scenarios, providing a comprehensive foundation for training and testing the model's
   performance across different network conditions.

· Data Preprocessing: Data preprocessing is a crucial step in preparing the dataset for ML models. Standard-
   Scaler was used to standardize the numerical feature values by removing the mean and scaling to unit vari-
   ance, which helps in achieving faster convergence during training. Label Encoding was applied to transform
   categorical variables into numerical representations, enabling ML algorithms to process them effectively.
   These preprocessing techniques ensure uniformity, reduce bias, and enhance the overall performance of the
   models.

· Data Balancing: The dataset was imbalanced, with certain classes being overrepresented. To address this
   issue, SMOTE TomekLInk (STL), Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) and KMeans-SMOTE (KMS) were
   utilized to balance the dataset by generating synthetic samples for minority classes. KMS leverages k-means
   clustering before applying SMOTE, ensuring better synthetic data generation by capturing intrinsic patterns
   in the data distribution.

· Dimensionality Reduction: Principal component analysis (PCA) was employed as the dimensionality re-
   duction technique. PCA reduces the feature space by projecting the data into a lower-dimensional subspace,
   retaining the most significant variance. This step minimizes redundancy and computational cost while pre-
   serving the critical information needed for intrusion detection.

· Classification Models: Random Forest Classifier (RFC) was chosen as the primary classification model due
   to its robustness, ability to handle high-dimensional data, and resistance to overfitting. The RFC model oper-
   ates by constructing multiple decision trees during training and combining their outputs to improve predic-
   tion accuracy. For comparison, other models, including Decision Tree Classifier (DTC), Gradient Boosting
   models (CBC, XGBC, LGBC, HGBC), were also evaluated.

· Performance Metrics: The intrusion detection performance of the models was evaluated using standard met-
   rics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, f1-score, Kappa, MCC, ROC-AUC. MAE, MSE, RMSE, Confusion
   Matrix and Classification Report. Additionally, computational complexity was assessed by recording training
   and prediction times for each model.

· Experimental Validation: The proposed methodology, integrating KMS, PCA, and RFC, was compared
   against existing works utilizing different data balancing processing and dimension reduction techniques with
   K-fold (K=5) cross validation. The results demonstrated that the proposed approach significantly outper-
   forms others in terms of performance metrics and computational efficiency, validating its effectiveness for IoT
   network anomaly detection tasks.This end-to-end methodology ensures balanced data, reduced dimension-
   ality, and high intrusion detection accuracy, making it well-suited for real-world applications in IoT network
   security.

Dataset description
The WSN-DS (Wireless Sensor Network Dataset) is a specialized benchmark for evaluating IDS in WSN
environments31. It simulates realistic WSN traffic, including both normal and malicious activities, such
as blackhole attacks, grayhole attacks, and flooding. The dataset is designed to reflect the constraints and
characteristics of WSNs, such as limited bandwidth, energy consumption, and dynamic topology. WSN-DS
provides a balanced representation of normal and attack behaviors, facilitating the development and testing of
ML-based IDS models. Its features include packet-level data and communication patterns, enabling detailed
analysis for intrusion detection. The dataset is critical for designing IDS tailored to the unique challenges of
WSNs, ensuring security while maintaining network efficiency and resource optimization.

    The TON-IoT Network dataset is a prominent benchmark designed to evaluate Intrusion Detection Systems
(IDS) in modern IoT networks32. It is derived from realistic IoT network scenarios, incorporating telemetry
data from IoT devices, network traffic, and logs from operating systems. The dataset includes a variety of attack
types, such as Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS), data exfiltration, backdoor, and password attacks, alongside
normal traffic, making it highly diverse and comprehensive. The dataset is notable for its high dimensionality
and heterogeneity, reflecting the dynamic and complex nature of IoT networks. It offers an extensive range of
features, enabling the evaluation of ML models for intrusion detection. TON-IoT Network dataset is a critical
resource for researchers aiming to enhance the scalability, robustness, and accuracy of IDS models in addressing
the security challenges of large-scale IoT ecosystems.

StandardScaler
The StandardScaler is a preprocessing technique used to normalize features by removing the mean and scaling
to unit variance. It ensures that the features have a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1, making the data
well-suited for ML algorithms sensitive to feature magnitudes. The standardization of a feature x is computed as:

for distance-based models, such as clustering algorithms, where feature scaling directly impacts the calculation

Data balancing (DB) techniques

Data Balancing (DB) techniques aim to address the class imbalance problem in datasets by ensuring an even
distribution of instances across all classes. This is crucial for improving the performance of ML models,
particularly in IDS, where the occurrence of attacks (minority classes) is often much rarer than normal behavior
(majority class). Data balancing techniques work by generating new samples for the minority classes or by
modifying the dataset to reduce bias toward majority classes. These methods either synthesize new data points
or combine existing ones to create balanced datasets.

    The class imbalance ratio can be calculated as:

                                                                           IR  =  Size  of  Majority  Class
                                                                                  Size  of  Minority  Class (3)

where a high imbalance ratio (IR) indicates severe class imbalance. DB techniques aim to minimize this ratio.

Class imbalance leads to biased models that perform poorly on minority classes, which are often the critical
classes for intrusion detection. Balancing the dataset ensures fair learning, reduces overfitting, and improves the
recall and precision of IDS models. Balancing techniques significantly enhance the accuracy and robustness of
IDS models by addressing the imbalance issue. They help prevent overfitting to the majority class, enabling the
model to detect rare but critical intrusion patterns more effectively.

STL--SMOTETomekLink
SMOTETomekLink (STL) is a hybrid approach combining the Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique
(SMOTE) and Tomek Link undersampling to handle both overfitting and class overlap issues in imbalanced
datasets. SMOTE generates synthetic samples for the minority class by interpolating between existing instances.
Tomek Link identifies and removes overlapping samples from majority and minority classes, enhancing data
separation.

    SMOTE generates new samples using:

where xi and xj are minority class samples, and  is a random number between 0 and 1.

GAN--generative adversarial network
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are DL-based methods that generate synthetic samples for the
minority class using a generator and discriminator model33. The generator creates synthetic samples, while the
discriminator evaluates their authenticity against real samples. The generator learns to produce realistic samples
that the discriminator cannot distinguish from real data.

    The optimization goal for GAN is:

                                     min  max              ExPdata  [log  D(x)]  +  EzPz  [log(1  -  D(G(z)))](5)
                                       G    D

where G is the generator, D is the discriminator, x represents real data, and z is a noise vector.

GANs are effective in generating complex and realistic data distributions, making them suitable for highly
imbalanced datasets. GANs create high-quality synthetic samples for minority classes, improving the ability of
IDS models to detect rare intrusions without overfitting.

KMeansSMOTE is a clustering-based oversampling technique that applies K-Means clustering to partition data
before generating synthetic samples using SMOTE. The dataset is first clustered using K-Means, and SMOTE
is applied within each cluster to generate synthetic samples for the minority class. This localized oversampling
preserves the original data distribution and reduces noise.

KMS overcomes the limitations of global oversampling by focusing on local distributions within each cluster,
generating more representative synthetic samples. KMS improves the balance and quality of IDS datasets, leading
to more accurate and robust intrusion detection. By preserving data distribution, it reduces the risk of overfitting
and enhances detection rates for minority classes. In this study, we propose using KMS for data balancing.
Its ability to cluster data before generating synthetic samples ensures that the data distribution is preserved,
and noise is minimized. This approach makes it particularly well-suited for improving the performance of IDS

Dimension reduction using PCA

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a statistical technique used for reducing the dimensionality of a dataset
by transforming it into a new coordinate system, where the axes (principal components) represent directions of
maximum variance in the data. PCA works by finding the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
of the data. The eigenvectors define the principal components, and the corresponding eigenvalues measure the
variance along these components. PCA transforms the data into a lower-dimensional space by selecting a subset
of components that capture the most variance.

    The covariance matrix is computed as:

                                                                      i=1

where xi is a data point, µ is the mean vector, and n is the number of samples.

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are computed as:
                                                         Cvi = ivi(8)

where i is an eigenvalue, and vi is the corresponding eigenvector.

PCA reduces the curse of dimensionality in WSN datasets, enabling faster and more efficient ML model training.
It eliminates redundant features and noise, improving the generalization ability of IDS models in wireless sensor
networks. By focusing on key variations in the data, PCA also enhances model interpretability and reduces
computational overhead.

Kaiser criterion
The Kaiser Criterion is a rule for selecting the number of principal components in PCA. It states that only
components with eigenvalues greater than 1 should be retained, as these components explain more variance than
an individual feature in the original dataset.

    Process: 1. Compute the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix after applying PCA. 2. Retain only those
components whose eigenvalues are greater than 1. 3. Transform the data using the selected components.

    The number of components selected by the Kaiser Criterion is given by:

where i is the eigenvalue of the i-th component, k is the total number of components, and 1(·) is the indicator

The Kaiser Criterion ensures that only the most significant principal components are retained, reducing
computational complexity without compromising the model's performance. For WSN datasets, this approach
helps in efficiently capturing key variations, enhancing the accuracy of IDS models while maintaining scalability
in resource-constrained environments.

    The Kaiser Criterion is widely used because it provides a simple yet effective heuristic to select components
that contribute meaningfully to the variance. For IDS datasets, the high-dimensional nature and the need to
focus on features with significant explanatory power make eigenvalue  1 a reasonable threshold.

    PCA efficiently reduces the high-dimensional feature space while retaining the most significant information.
By eliminating redundant and less informative features using kaiser criterion, PCA minimizes noise and
improves computational efficiency. This streamlined feature set allows the classifier to focus on the most relevant
patterns, enhancing its predictive accuracy and performance metrics.

Machine learning (ML) algorithms

Machine Learning (ML) algorithms are computational techniques used to build models that can learn patterns
from data and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed. These algorithms are crucial
for tasks such as detection attacks in IDS for Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs).

Decision Tree Classifier (DTC)
A Decision Tree Classifier (DTC) is a tree-like structure where each internal node represents a feature (or
attribute), each branch represents a decision rule, and each leaf node represents a class label. The algorithm
splits the dataset into subsets based on feature values, recursively creating branches to partition the data into
homogenous subsets.

    The decision tree algorithm uses the following recursive rule for splitting:

Here, H(S) is the entropy of the dataset, and Sv is the subset of data for each value v of the feature f .

DTC is easy to interpret and visualize, making it suitable for IDS models. It effectively handles both numerical
and categorical data and is less prone to overfitting compared to other algorithms, especially when pruned.

Random Forest Classifier (RFC)
A Random Forest Classifier (RFC) is an ensemble learning method that builds multiple decision trees using
different subsets of the data and features. Each tree makes an independent prediction, and the final output is
determined by aggregating the predictions (usually by majority voting for classification).

    The output of RFC can be represented as:

                                                     y^RFC  =  1  T
                                                               T       y^t(12)

                                                                  t=1

where T is the total number of trees, and y^t is the prediction from the t-th tree.

RFC is robust to overfitting, handles large datasets well, and improves prediction accuracy by reducing variance
through bagging. It is also less sensitive to noisy data and outliers.

CatBoost Classifier (CBC)
The CatBoost Classifier (CBC) is an advanced gradient boosting algorithm designed for classification tasks.
It is based on the gradient boosting framework but has several key optimizations to improve performance,
reduce overfitting, and handle categorical features efficiently. CatBoost stands for "Categorical Boosting" and is
particularly useful when the dataset contains categorical variables, as it can process them directly without the
need for manual preprocessing, such as one-hot encoding.

    The general form of the gradient boosting equation for CatBoost is as follows:

where F (x) is the prediction function. m is the learning rate for the m-th boosting round. hm(x) is the weak
learner (usually a decision tree) fitted in the m-th iteration. M is the total number of boosting iterations.

In CatBoost, the decision trees hm(x) are built using a gradient-based optimization process that minimizes a
loss function, typically the log-loss or binary cross-entropy in classification problems.

    With techniques like ordered boosting and regularization, CatBoost reduces overfitting and ensures better
generalization on unseen data. Moreover, it requires fewer hyperparameters, making it faster to train compared
to other gradient boosting algorithms, and is robust against noisy data and outliers.

Extreme Gradient Boosting Classifier (XGBC)
Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBC) is an advanced implementation of gradient boosting that optimizes model
training by using decision trees in a boosting framework. XGBC builds trees sequentially, where each tree
corrects the errors of the previous one. It uses regularization to prevent overfitting and improve generalization.

    The final prediction in XGBC is given by:

                                                     T
                                                     y^ = tft(x)(14)

                                                                                          t=1

where t is the weight of the t-th tree and ft(x) is the prediction of the t-th tree.

XGBC is highly efficient and scalable, providing high predictive accuracy. It is robust against overfitting due to
its regularization mechanism and is widely used in IDS due to its ability to handle large datasets and complex
relationships.

LightGBM Classifier (LGBC)
LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine) is a gradient boosting framework that uses a histogram-based
approach for faster training. It builds decision trees leaf-wise (as opposed to level-wise), which helps reduce the
number of splits and improves computational efficiency.

    The prediction in LGBC can be written as:

                                                     T
                                                     y^ = tft(x)(15)

                                                                                          t=1

Similar to XGBC, but with improved efficiency and speed due to the histogram-based approach.

LGBC is faster and more memory-efficient than traditional gradient boosting algorithms, making it suitable
for large-scale IDS datasets. It can handle categorical features directly and achieves high accuracy in a shorter
training time.

Histogram-based Gradient Boosting Classifier (HGBC)
Histogram-based Gradient Boosting (HGBC) builds trees using gradient boosting and bins continuous features
into histograms, which reduces the number of calculations during tree construction. This makes it efficient,
especially for large datasets.

    Similar to LightGBM, HGBC's prediction is given by:

                                                     T
                                                     y^ = tft(x)(16)

                                                                                          t=1

where ft(x) is the decision function of the t-th tree, and t is the corresponding weight.

HGBC offers high accuracy and efficiency, with a focus on memory usage optimization, making it well-suited
for IDS in resource-constrained environments. It allows for fast model training and is capable of handling high-
dimensional data with ease.

Result analysis

Result analysis plays a crucial role in evaluating the effectiveness of IDS built using ML models. It involves
assessing the ability of the model to accurately classify intrusion events while ensuring minimal false positives
and false negatives. This analysis typically includes metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, f1-score, and area
under the curve (AUC), which offer insights into the model's overall performance and robustness. The results
obtained help in comparing different models and selecting the one with the best performance for deployment
in real-world scenarios.

                                  Potential impact of PCA on performance
                                      The high dimensionality and heterogeneity of the WSN-DS dataset indeed necessitate the use of dimensionality
                                      reduction techniques to optimize model performance and interpretability. Principal Component Analysis
                                      (PCA), applied alongside the Kaiser Criterion, plays a pivotal role in this context. PCA reduces the dataset's
                                      dimensionality by transforming it into a set of orthogonal components that maximize variance. This approach
                                      eliminates redundancy, minimizes noise, and mitigates the curse of dimensionality, which can hinder efficient
                                      training of IDS. In our study, PCA was applied to WSN-DS, retaining components with eigenvalues greater
                                      than 1, as per the Kaiser Criterion. This ensured that only the most significant features were used for training,
                                      reducing computational overhead while preserving over 95% of the data's variance. By streamlining the feature
                                      space, PCA enabled faster training and inference without sacrificing model accuracy (99.94%), which achieved a
                                      remarkable ROC-AUC of 99.99% in combination with the Hybrid (KMS+PCA+RFC) framework (Tables 6 and
                                      15). Additionally, PCA improved the model's generalization capability by focusing on key variations, reducing
                                      overfitting risks. The interpretability of results was further enhanced as PCA allowed us to identify the most
                                      impactful features in intrusion detection scenarios (Figs. 2, 3). By demonstrating the benefits of PCA and the
                                      Kaiser Criterion, we highlight how dimensionality reduction not only boosts IDS performance but also ensures
                                      scalability and reliability, which are critical for resource-constrained wireless sensor network environments.

Conclusion

In this research, we proposed a novel intrusion detection system (IDS) for wireless sensor networks (WSNs) by
integrating K-means Smote (KMS) with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Random Forest Classifier
(RFC). Our approach aimed at improving the performance and efficiency of IDS by addressing the challenges of
class imbalance and dimensionality reduction in real-time applications. The experimental results demonstrated
that our proposed model outperforms existing state-of-the-art models, achieving higher accuracy, precision,
recall, and f1-score across different datasets, including WSN-DS and TON-IoT Network. The comparative
analysis showed that KMS+PCA+RFC delivers superior classification performance, with the ability to effectively
handle large-scale datasets and imbalanced classes. Moreover, the complexity analysis revealed that our model
strikes a balance between classification accuracy and computational efficiency, making it suitable for deployment
in resource-constrained environments such as WSNs. The time efficiency of our model was competitive,
showing minimal increases in training and prediction times compared to other models. Overall, our research
contributes to advancing IDS in WSNs, providing a robust and scalable solution for intrusion detection in IoT-
based networks, and offering valuable insights for future work in this domain, particularly in optimizing the
trade-off between model complexity and real-time application performance.

    Despite the promising results of our proposed model, there are certain limitations that need to be addressed.
First, the performance of the model relies on the quality of the feature extraction techniques, which may vary
across different network conditions and types of attacks. Second, our approach does not yet account for the
dynamic nature of wireless sensor networks, where environmental factors and changing network conditions
could affect the model's privacy and accuracy.

    For future work, we aim to explore adaptive feature selection methods that can dynamically adjust based on
real-time network conditions. Furthermore, we plan to investigate federated learning techniques to improve the
privacy of our model in large-scale, distributed WSN environments.
