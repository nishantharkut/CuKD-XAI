F. MODEL’S EXPLAINIBILITY
In this research work, the use of Explainable AI (XAI) tech-
niques like LIME and SHAP allowed for the determination
of each feature’s importance and provided explanations for
the impact of every feature on specific decisions through
both local and global representations. Local representation
refers to explaining the influence of individual features on
a particular test set, accomplished using LIME, SHAP force
plots, and SHAP waterfall plots. Global representation, on the
other hand, explains the importance of features across a group
of test sets. This was demonstrated using SHAP summary
plots, SHAP summary plots with violin distributions, and
force plots encompassing all test data.
1) EXPLAINABILITY OF THE OUTCOME USING LIME (LOCAL)
LIME plots provide a clear visual explanation of each
feature’s positive and negative impact on decision-making,
offering deeper insights into why a particular decision was
made by the proposed model. Figure 10 presents the LIME
plot for the top eight most impactful features, showing their
influence on determining whether the network is normal
or under attack at a specific instance. A horizontal bar
that extends to the right and is colored green in the plot
indicates a feature’s beneficial influence on the decision-
making process. On the other hand, a red-colored bar that
extends to the left indicates a negative influence. These bars’
lengths indicate how much of an impact each feature has,
making it easy to see how much of a contribution each feature
contributes to the model’s conclusion.
According to Figure 10 of the LIME explanation, the
feature named ‘‘SCH_S’’ has the most significant impact
on the decision-making process for determining whether the
network is normal or under attack. Table 6 indicates that the
value of ‘‘SCH_S’’ is 13.00. However, the LIME explanation
