5) SHAP STACKED FORCE PLOT OF THE TEST SET (GLOBAL)
Supervised clustering of force plots, also known as stacked
force plots, is a technique for understanding how various
characteristics affect a model’s predictions and for visualizing
patterns and trends in the model’s output. The vertical axis of
this kind of plot shows the cumulative effect of characteristics
on the decisions made by the model, while the horizontal
axis represents the test samples. This kind of plot is a two-
dimensional representation. You may detect trends and the
relative importance of factors in influencing the model’s
results by stacking the force plots to see how specific features
consistently affect predictions across numerous samples.
Figure 16 illustrates the output probability versus the
test samples for 200 test instances. The test samples are
represented by the horizontal axis, and the output probability
is displayed by the vertical axis. From the plot, a clear
dominance of the feature labeled ‘‘ADV_S’’ is evident.
Initially, this feature had a slightly negative influence on the
output, but as the samples progress, the feature increasingly
shifts towards a significant positive influence, strongly
affecting the model’s final predictions.
The effects of the feature named ‘‘ADV_S’’ can be
explicitly understood from Figure 17. The plot shows that
while the feature had a maximum negative impact on the
output, its impact was consistent throughout the test samples.
This consistency highlights the importance of ‘‘ADV_S’’ as
a critical feature for the model’s prediction capabilities, even
though its influence was primarily negative.
The results from both LIME and SHAP demonstrate a clear

enhances the reliability of the model’s explanations, as the

H. STRENGTH, LIMITATION, AND FUTURE WORK
The model developed in this research demonstrates consis-
tently high accuracy, supported by extensive refinement and
validation through a Streamlit web application. This per-
formance highlights its effectiveness in identifying various
attack types and normal conditions within the dataset. A key
strength of our ensemble model is its high accuracy, achieved
through PSO-tuned hyperparameters that outperform existing
models. The integration of XAI techniques, such as SHAP
and LIME, enhances interpretability, making the model
practical for real-world applications by offering insights
into its decision-making process. Additionally, our approach
effectively addresses dataset imbalance and reduces false
positives, allowing for scalability in large networks with di-
verse traffic patterns. This is particularly beneficial in critical
domains like military surveillance, healthcare monitoring,
and industrial automation, where real-time and accurate
detection of security threats is essential. The application
of SMOTE-Tomek for dataset balancing ensures better
generalization across varying network conditions. Despite
these strengths, the model has limitations primarily related
to computational complexity. The ensemble of machine
learning algorithms and optimization techniques increases
computational overhead, potentially limiting real-time appli-
cability in resource-constrained environments. Furthermore,
the model’s performance has been evaluated on specific
datasets, and its generalization to other domains remains
to be fully explored. The model has not been tested on
dynamically changing datasets, where it may require different
