Salmi and Oughdir [1] and Chandre et al. [16] both adopted
deep learning-based approaches, implementing Convolu-
tional Neural Networks (CNN) for enhanced classification.
In addition to CNN, one of the studies also experimented
with Deep Neural Networks (DNN) and Recurrent Neural
Networks (RNN). However, these models only managed
to achieve around 97% accuracy. Moreover, deep learning
models typically require extensive computational resources.
Given that several ML models can achieve better performance
with less computational effort, the necessity for these deep
learning approaches becomes less compelling.
Jeevaraj et al. [39] proposed a different approach, focusing
on feature selection rather than solely relying on classifiers.
Their method involved iteratively removing less important
features and re-evaluating the model’s performance after
each iteration. While this approach thoroughly explored
feature importance, reducing the number of features did
not significantly improve the model’s performance. Naive
Bayes was used as the classifier, and the model achieved its
best accuracy score of 95.82% using the six most important
features.
Elsadig [21] also emphasized feature selection as a
strategy to achieve better results with reduced computational
effort. In this study, the Gini feature selection method was
employed alongside the Decision Tree classifier. The Gini
method selected 16 out of 18 features from the dataset.
However, given the highly imbalanced nature of the dataset,
incorporating additional preprocessing steps could have
enhanced the model’s reliability and robustness for real-world
applications. Despite the absence of such steps, the model still
achieved a commendable accuracy of 99.5%.
The best-performing model from previous works was
developed by Nguyen et al. [25], who used GSWO for
hyperparameter tuning and feature selection, combined with
the CatBoost classifier for classification tasks. The model
demonstrated excellent performance, achieving an accuracy
of 99.62%. However, since the dataset was imbalanced,
additional steps to prevent the overfitting of specific classes
were necessary. Moreover, the computational demands of the
GSWO optimizer were quite high. Despite these challenges,
the model’s performance was outstanding. Additionally, XAI
methods such as SHAP and LIME, which were not used in
prior works, offer a distinct advantage in our model. These
techniques allow us to provide interpretable insights into
the model’s decision-making process, giving both local and
global explanations of feature importance. This transparency
not only increases the trustworthiness of the model but also
enables a deeper understanding of why certain predictions
are made—an aspect often missing in black-box models like
deep learning approaches used by other authors. Moreover,
our approach integrates PSO for hyperparameter tuning,
