Nguyen et al. [25] introduced a unique strategy called
Genetic Sacrificial Whale Optimization (GSWO) to address
and overcome existing methods of intrusion detection. In ad-
dition to the efficient quantization of the GSWO algorithm,
classification was performed using a CatBoost model.
In the WSN-DS, WSNBFSF, NSL-KDD, and CICIDS2017
datasets, the GSWO-CatBoost approach has above 90%
accuracy and has been reported to be faster than deep learning
methods.
An intrusion detection system (IDS) tailored for software-
defined 5G networks was proposed by Bocu et al. [26].
The solution leverages the scalability and adaptability of
software-defined networks (SDN) for efficient implementa-
tion by integrating artificial intelligence models to detect
intrusions that were previously undetected. The system’s
accuracy falls short of some of the most advanced models
in the field, despite its practical usefulness for real-world
deployment in 5G contexts.
Despite significant advancements in IDS for WSN, several
research gaps remain. First, many existing models are limited
by dataset imbalances, which affect detection accuracy
and lead to high false-positive rates. While techniques
like SMOTE improve class balance, the performance gains
are often marginal. Second, many approaches, including
optimization algorithms like PSO and Firefly, have demon-
strated high accuracy but tend to suffer from computational
inefficiency, scalability issues, or limited generalization
across diverse datasets. Furthermore, while deep learning
models have shown promise, their high complexity and
computational demands restrict their practicality for real-
time applications. Lastly, intrusion prevention, especially
in dynamic and large-scale WSN environments, remains
underexplored, with a focus predominantly on detection
rather than preemptive measures to block evolving attacks.
Addressing these gaps requires the development of more
efficient, scalable, and adaptive models capable of handling
dynamic network conditions and real-world attack scenarios.
III. METHODOLOGY
In this study, a comprehensive approach to intrusion detection
in WSN is presented, utilizing a robust ML model that has
been optimized for performance and interpretability through
advanced data processing and ensemble techniques. The
dataset, known as WSN-DS, contains diverse real-world
network scenarios and attack conditions. Preprocessing of
the dataset involved converting categorical variables into
numerical forms for compatibility with the machine learning
model. To address the class imbalance, SMOTE was applied
to balance the dataset. The data was split into 80% for training
and 20% for testing. Hyperparameter tuning was performed
using PSO to enhance model performance, as it proved to be
highly efficient and emerged as the most effective technique
compared to other optimization techniques. Various machine
learning methods were employed in the process of finding
best outcome, and at the end combining different algorithms
proved to be effective. Thus, an ensemble model comprising
RF, DT, and KNN algorithms was employed, with its
effectiveness evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation. Metrics
such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score were used to
assess the model’s performance. SHAP and LIME methods
were incorporated to provide global and local explanations
of the model’s behavior.
A. DATASET DESCRIPTION
A dataset called WSN-DS by Almomani et al. [41] is intended
for the identification of DoS assaults, comprising 374,661
records, with 9% identified as DoS attacks. The LEACH
system, a popular hierarchical routing system in Wireless
Sensor Networks (WSNs), was used to curate this dataset,
which covers typical network behaviors and four different
kinds of DoS assaults (Grayhole, Blackhole, TDMA, and
Flooding). Network Simulator 2 (NS-2) was used to gather
data, and after processing, 23 features were produced, adding
value to the dataset. Researchers looking to create strategies
for mitigating DoS attacks and improving network security
will find this dataset to be a useful resource [27].
B. DATA PREPROCESSING
Data preprocessing, which involves cleaning and transform-
ing raw data to ensure its quality and suitability for analysis,
is an essential stage in the construction of models. This
process is vital for the model’s effectiveness, particularly in
achieving successful detection in Wireless Sensor Networks
(WSN) and ensuring data compatibility with ML models. The
subsequent section will detail the specific preprocessing steps
applied to the dataset.
1) MANUAL FEATURE SELECTION
Manual feature selection is the identification of features
for incorporation into a machine learning model based on
domain expertise, intuition, or exploratory data analysis,
rather than depending on automated techniques. This method
depends on human discernment to identify and eliminate
features considered unnecessary, redundant, or unhelpful for
augmenting the model’s prediction efficacy. In the WSN
dataset, the feature labeled ’ id’ was considered extraneous
for attack detection, as it failed to demonstrate any patterns
linked to assault scenarios. As a result, this feature was
eliminated during the preprocessing stage. Omitting this
feature improves the model’s effectiveness by decreasing the
amount of features it must analyze, enabling it to concentrate
exclusively on the most pertinent data.
