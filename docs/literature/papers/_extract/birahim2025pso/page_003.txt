Abhale and Reddy [14] conducted a comparative examina-
tion of various deep learning-based intrusion detection sys-
tems and presented updated methods, leading to performance
improvements. In particular, the accuracy of the CNN-based
algorithm went from 99.39% to 99.42%. Other models that
shown notable improvements were those that were based
on RNN, LSTM, and GRU. The authors emphasized that
while deep learning is generally applied to large and complex
datasets, its key advantage in this context is the reduction in
human effort and improved feature correlation.
An IDS framework combining Cuckoo Search Greedy
Optimization (CSGO) and Likelihood Support Vector Ma-
chine (LSVM) models was proposed by Hemanand et al.
[15]. The system’s accuracy and efficiency were improved
by the LSVM component. Using popular network datasets
like NSL-KDD and UNSW-NB15, the model was verified
and was able to detect network assaults with a peak accuracy
of 99.65%.
A deep learning-based intrusion prevention model was
proposed by Chandre et al. [16], who achieved 97%
accuracy through laborious computations utilizing a CNN-
based methodology. The model established specific rules
for each class, resulting in improved class separation. The
research concluded that the model’s performance remained
consistent across datasets of varying sizes, suggesting that
it had effectively learned features, making it adaptable and
robust regardless of dataset size.

A two-stage intrusion detection system based on deep
neural
networks
was
proposed
by
Gowdhaman
and
Dhanapal [18]. The model employed a cross-correlation
approach in the first step to choose the best features, and
then in the second stage, it applied a deep neural network to
identify intrusions. With an accuracy of 95.53%, the system
outperformed a number of well-known models. Nevertheless,
other deep learning-based intrusion detection systems have
shown higher performance in spite of this one. This model
was deemed too inefficient for general use due to the inherent
difficulties and constraints of deep learning.
An intelligent hybrid model was proposed by Mohamed
Behiry and Aly [19], which combined the K-means clustering
model enhanced with information gain (KMC-IG) for feature
extraction with feature reduction techniques like Singular
Value Decomposition (SVD) and Principal Component
Analysis (PCA) to reduce the number of features. Several
datasets, including NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB 15, and CICIDS
2017, were used to validate the model. The main driving
force for the entire study was feature reduction, which
improved the model’s performance from 98.9% to 99.7% in
accuracy.
Notable results were obtained with the hybrid ML models
for intrusion detection proposed by Gebremariam et al.
[20]. Their work demonstrated a 99.80% accuracy on the
NSL-KDD dataset using a hybrid Random Forest and
Extreme Gradient Boost (RF-XGB) model, and a perfect
100% accuracy on the UNSW-NB15 and CICIDS2017
datasets with a hybrid Cluster Labeling K-Means (CLK-M)
model. The overall accuracy across all hybrid models was
99.46%. The model is reliable, but the authors note that it does
not completely remove the possibility of assaults on Internet
of Things devices.
The fundamental goal of Elsadig [21] model was to achieve
faster processing times without sacrificing competitive ac-
curacy. The model achieved a detection accuracy of 99.5%
and outperformed popular classifiers like Random Forest
(RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and k-Nearest
Neighbor (KNN) by lowering processing time to just 9.7%,
13%, and 2% of their respective times. The model was trained
on a single dataset, which the author points out as a significant
restriction. She suggests that using many datasets could result
in a more effective and versatile model that is better suited for
a range of circumstances.
In the context of WSNs, Karthikeyan et al. [22] suggested
an intrusion detection system based on the firefly algorithm,
examining the algorithm’s potential for security-focused
optimization methods. The firefly approach specifically used
a support vector machine (SVM) model in conjunction with
a grey wolf optimizer (GWO) for intrusion detection. At its
peak accuracy of 99.34%, the model was able to utilize the
firefly method.
A model specially developed for denial-of-service assaults
was proposed by Liu et al. [23], and it was based on the
k-nearest neighbor algorithm and the arithmetic optimization
technique. In order to optimize the model’s accuracy,
population communication was improved through the use of
a parallel method, and the optimization was adjusted using
the Lévy flight strategy. These methods enabled the model to
gain an approximate accuracy rate of 99%.
