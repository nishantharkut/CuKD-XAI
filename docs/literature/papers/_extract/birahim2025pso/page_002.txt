An Intrusion Detection System (IDS) is a software or hard-
ware solution designed to detect and prevent unauthorized
access to computer systems or networks [4]. IDS can be
categorized into two types: host-based, which is integrated
into a host device to monitor processes and user activity
locally for intrusion detection, and network-based, which is
the more common type. Network-based IDS operates across
a network, monitoring traffic flow for potential intrusions
in a distributed manner [5]. IDS classifies the Aegean
Wi-Fi Intrusion Dataset (AWID) into four primary categories
based on network behavior: Normal, Flooding, Injection, and
Impersonation. Intrusion detection in IDS can be divided into
four methods:
Anomaly-based IDS, which is effective for detecting
unknown attacks by identifying abnormal behavior; Misuse-
based (or signature-based) IDS, which detects known attacks
by matching them with predefined signatures; Specification-
based IDS, which monitors network components like routing
tables, nodes, and protocols based on rules and thresholds
to identify irregularities; Hybrid-based IDS, which com-
bines anomaly, signature, and specification-based techniques.
Wireless networks have surpassed wired networks in terms
of flexibility, accessibility, mobility, and the lack of need
for additional infrastructure [6]. Wireless Intrusion Detec-
tion Systems (WIDS) are specialized sensors designed to
monitor and protect Wireless Local Area Networks (WLAN)
by continuously scanning all operating frequencies within
WLAN to detect and respond to intrusions [11]. The design
goals of WSN are centered on improving data quality,
reducing response time, enhancing network security, and
extending network lifetime. Anomaly detection is critical
for achieving these objectives, as abnormal data inevitably
arises due to various uncontrollable factors. Such data can
increase network traffic, delay, and waste limited energy
resources [10], while also compromising subsequent data
analysis and processing. Hence, timely and effective detec-
tion of anomalies is essential [7]. Despite the development
of some solutions, challenges remain in terms of detection
accuracy and speed. Leveraging artificial intelligence (AI)
presents a promising avenue for overcoming these technical
limitations. One significant challenge in WSN intrusion
detection is the imbalanced nature of datasets, where normal
instances vastly outnumber intrusion instances, negatively
affecting detection accuracy. Moreover, WSNs often consist
of a large number of sensor nodes, which leads to increased
computational complexity and scalability issues [8]. High
false positive rates and inadequate adaptability to evolving
attack techniques further complicate intrusion detection [9].
To address these challenges, we propose a model that
combines Machine Learning (ML) techniques with the
Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique and Tomek
Links (SMOTE-Tomek). This approach synthesizes minority
intrusion instances while removing Tomek links, thereby
balancing the dataset and improving detection accuracy and
reliability. Our model represents a significant improvement
over existing methods, providing an optimal solution for
WSN intrusion detection.
The primary contributions of this study are as follows:
1) Developed a cutting-edge Intrusion Detection System
(IDS) for Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) that
leverages Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for hy-
perparameter tuning in combination with an ensemble
machine learning approach, significantly enhancing
detection accuracy, adaptability, and model perfor-
mance by efficiently optimizing parameter settings for
better accuracy and generalization.
2) Implemented the SMOTE-Tomek technique to over-
come the significant challenge of imbalanced datasets,
leading to improved detection of minority attack
classes in WSNs.
3) Incorporated state-of-the-art explainability tools such
as LIME(Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Expla-
nations) and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
to provide both local and global interpretations of the
model’s decisions, enhancing transparency and trust in
its predictions.
4) Developed an interactive Streamlit web application to
visualize the model’s predictions and demonstrate its
performance across different attack types and normal
conditions in WSNs, making the system accessible and
easy to interpret for users.
5) Utilized a robust ensemble of ML models includ-
ing Random Forest (RF), Decision Tree (DT), and
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), with soft voting to
maximize the strength of multiple classifiers and
deliver more reliable intrusion detection.
II. LITERATURE REVIEW
The topic of safety in wireless sensor networks has attracted
a lot of interest lately. Various ML-based algorithms have
been developed to stop network attacks or compromises. This
section discusses a few of these models.
Dharini et al. [12] created their dataset using MATLAB
with the main goal of figuring out how many characteristics
would maximize model performance. Their findings indi-
cated that peak performance was achieved using 14 features,
resulting in an accuracy of 99.16% with both the LightGBM
and XGBoost models, and 99.8% with the bagging method.
Their study further demonstrated that reducing the number
of features led to a decline in model performance, as models
with only 5 or 3 features performed significantly worse.
Notably, the LightGBM model was recognized for being the
fastest, owing to its distinctive capabilities.
