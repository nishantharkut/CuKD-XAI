The TABLE 5 compares the performance of a classification
model across different types of cyber-attacks under two
data conditions: imbalanced and balanced. Key metrics like
precision, recall, and F1 score reveal the model’s accuracy in
identifying attack classes. Precision reflects the correctness
of identified attacks, while recall measures the detection of
actual attacks. The F1 score balances both metrics.
In the imbalanced data scenario, attack classes like
Browser Hijacking, Recon-Ping Sweep, and XSS showed
very low performance, with precision, recall, and F1 scores
near zero, indicating frequent misclassification and rare
detection due to under-representation. This scarcity led the
model to focus on more common attack types, resulting
in bias and significant misclassification of these minority
classes. However, when the data was balanced, performance
improved substantially across most attack types, particularly
in recall. For instance, Backdoor Malware saw its recall
increase from 0.500 to 0.999, while Browser Hijacking
and Recon-Ping Sweep, previously undetected, improved
to recalls of 0.962 and 0.961, respectively. Balancing the
data enabled the model to better identify these attacks by
providing a more representative sample for each class and
reducing bias toward majority classes. In the imbalanced
data scenario, certain attack classes like Browser Hijacking,
Recon-Ping Sweep, and XSS show very low performance,
with scores near zero across precision, recall, and F1 metrics.
This suggests that these attacks were often misclassified or
rarely detected, likely due to their under-representation in
the imbalanced dataset. This scarcity led the model to focus
on more frequent attack types, resulting in a strong bias
and significant misclassification of these minority classes.
When the data was balanced, performance on most attack
types improved substantially, especially in recall, indicating
a higher rate of correctly identified attacks across classes. For
instance, Backdoor Malware improved in recall from 0.500 to
0.999, while Browser Hijacking and Recon-Ping Sweep,
previously undetected (with recalls of 0.000), saw a recall
boost to 0.962 and 0.961, respectively. Balancing the data
helped the model better identify these attacks by providing
a more representative sample for each class, reducing its bias
toward majority classes and enabling it to generalize more
effectively.
TABLE 7 shows our TICNN model achieves accuracy
comparable to state-of-the-art methods [5], [12], [23], [24] on
CICIDS2017 and CICIoT2023 datasets. While other models
reach high accuracy, often through heavy synthetic data
generation that might introduce bias [5], [24], our approach
focuses on efficiency. By transforming data into 2D images,
TICNN significantly reduces both computational and detec-
tion times. This allows our model to deliver top-tier accuracy
with lower resource demands. Consequently, TICNN offers
a more practical and efficient solution for real-time intrusion
detection systems.
