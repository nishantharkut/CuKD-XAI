E. TIGAN: GENERATIVE QUALITY & STATISTICAL
VALIDATION
The reliability of the samples generated from the TIGAN is
assured through performing both qualitative and quantitative
evaluations on the samples created from this generator. Most
previous Work related to the generation of Synthetic Data
for use in Tabular ID systems has relied upon Classification
Accuracy determined after training as an indirect method for
assessing quality, however this method does not typically
identify mode collapse or low diversity of generated data.
The authors believe that this is the first effort which has
involved utilizing industry standard computer vision metrics
to confer reliability upon the quality of synthetic Tabular to
Image IoT Traffic Data, and therefore sets a new standard for
the evaluation of the Cleanliness of Synthetic Data generated
for use in Intrusion Detection Systems.
1) VISUAL FIDELITY
In the analysis we conducted of TIGAN’s output with
9 randomly selected sample images as shown in Fig. 2.,
we performed a side-by-side comparison against 9 synthetic
images generated through TIGAN. The results of our
visual inspection demonstrate that TIGAN can replicate the
complex textural patterns and color distributions seen in
actual network traffic imagery. Additionally, the synthesized
network traffic imagery show similar spatial correlations to
those of the actual minority class samples. This indicates that
TIGAN has learned to model the underlying feature manifold
of the minority class, instead of simply memorizing data
points from that class.
2) QUANTITATIVE METRICS (FID & IS)
We used the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception
Score (IS) to measure this fidelity objectively. While IS
assesses the diversity and clarity of the generated samples
(higher is better), FID measures the difference between the
feature distributions of real and synthetic images (lower is
better). According to our evaluation results, TIGAN’s FID
score of 11.24 was considerably lower than that of typical
GAN implementations, which typically range between 20 and
40 on complex datasets. Additionally, the model obtained a
Inception Score (IS) of 4.15, indicating that TIGAN does not
produce generic noise but rather a variety of attack patterns
that are sufficiently different for the classifier to discriminate.
This statistical validation demonstrates that high-quality,
statistically representative synthetic data are responsible for
the performance improvements seen in TICNN.
F. LIMITATIONS OF IMAGE-BASED IDS
An image created from a table-based dataset results in greater
efficiency, though processing effort is required to achieve
this benefit. For instance, packet size represents a field in a
table and has a specific interpretation based on the network
attribute it represents while the image does not maintain that
same reference. Therefore, referring to the image, a neural
network will still identify patterns, but tracking a predicted
output back to the network packet field that resulted in that
prediction is much more complex through a convolutional
neural network than it is when using a decision tree model.
Additionally, regarding the practical deployment of
TIGAN, there is a trade-off between the ‘offline’ training cost
and ‘online’ detection benefits. While training TIGAN for
minority subclasses requires approximately 360–800 seconds
per class, this is a one-time computational investment.
The resulting balanced TICNN model provides ultra-low
latency packet detection (1.5–2.9 ms), which is critical
for real-time IoT security. However, several limitations
remain. First, the current approach relies on feature-rich
network headers; its effectiveness may decrease when dealing
with heavily encrypted traffic where payload inspection is
impossible. Second, the model is currently designed for static
training cycles. In online learning scenarios, where new
IoT threats evolve dynamically, the system would require
periodic retraining or a more adaptive incremental learning
architecture to maintain its edge.
V. CONCLUSION
In summary, this research illustrates how converting large,
multidimensional IoT datasets into two-dimensional images
can help alleviate memory limitations by enabling four
times more effective data compression, thereby allowing
more rapid data processing. Furthermore, the use of the
TIGAN-generated hybrid sampling method combined with
a lightweight TICNN provides nearly perfect (over 99%)
macro-level classification across more than 33 attack types.
Therefore, this framework delivers a scalable real-time IDS
(Intrusion Detection System) with the capability to accurately
identify infrequent attacks while minimizing the impact on
IoT (Internet of Things) edge device resource constraints.
