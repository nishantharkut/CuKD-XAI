C. PROPOSED TRANSFORMED IMAGE CONVOLUTION
NEURAL NETWORK (TICNN) THAT RESOLVES RQ1
The proposed model for image classification consists of
convolutional layers with 64, 128, and 256 filters using ReLU
activation to extract features. Max pooling layers downsam-
ple feature maps, maintaining important details. A Global
Average Pooling layer reduces parameters, followed by a
fully connected layer consisting of 256 units paired with a
dropout layer set at a 0.5 rate to mitigate overfitting. The
model employs softmax activation along with categorical
cross-entropy loss to enhance classification performance and
optimization, with each component serving a specific pur-

IV. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION AND RESULTS
The experiments were conducted on a system equipped
with an AMD Ryzen Threadripper Pro processor featuring
12 cores, 62.7 GiB of memory, and an NVIDIA GeForce
RTX 3070 GPU. The primary dataset used for evaluation
is the CICIoT2023 dataset, which contains over 46 million
attack samples distributed across 33 attack classes and
1 benign class, as visualized in Fig. 6. The dataset is highly
imbalanced, posing a substantial challenge for accurate and
fair classification. This makes it well-suited for evaluating the
robustness and effectiveness of deep learning-based intrusion
detection systems. To further assess the generalizability of the
proposed model, the CICIDS2017 dataset was also employed.
CICIDS2017 comprises 14 attack classes and 1 benign
class represented in Fig. 7, offering a broad spectrum of
contemporary network threats including DDoS, PortScan,
