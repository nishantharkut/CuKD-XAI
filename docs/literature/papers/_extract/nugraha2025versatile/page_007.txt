3.2 Applied XAI techniques
Model explanations can be grouped into global and local
types: the former reveal overall model logic, whereas the
latter clarify speciﬁc decisions. As illustrated in Fig. 1, we
employ SHAP for both global and local explanations and
LIME solely for local ones. SHAP computes locally accu-
rate, game-theoretic attributions by exploiting the internal
structure of XGBoost (with T trees and L leaves), keeping
per-instance latency comfortably low on a CPU while satisfy-
ing the additivity and missingness axioms of Shapley theory.
LIME is completely model-agnostic; its perturb-and-ﬁt strat-
egy produces sparse linear explanations that many security
analysts ﬁnd intuitive and easier to act upon than dense gra-
dient heatmaps. Using both methods in tandem enables a
cross-validation step: if the top-ℓcontributing features dis-
agree, the instance is ﬂagged for further analyst scrutiny,
thereby improving trust in the explanations. Formally, we
compute a consistency coefﬁcient κ = |SSHAP∩SLIME|

Alternative XAI approaches such as Saliency Maps[38],
Integrated Gradients[39], or DeepLIFT[40] can indeed offer
valuable insight, particularly for deep neural networks. How-

whereas the SHAP–LIME pair delivers the necessary balance
of faithfulness, efﬁciency, and model-agnostic applicability
for tabular network-ﬂow data.
3.2.1 SHAP
SHAP, introduced by Lundberg and Lee [3], employs Shap-
ley values from game theory to quantify each feature’s
contribution to an AI model’s prediction, assigning values
that reﬂect each feature’s positive or negative impact. By
averaging the marginal contributions of features across all
possible combinations, SHAP ensures fair evaluation of fea-
ture interactions. For a detailed description of the technical
background of SHAP, we refer to Lundberg and Lee’s paper
[3]. SHAP’s advantages lie in its game-theoretical founda-
tion and properties: Symmetry (equal importance for equal
contributors), Dummy (zero contribution for non-inﬂuential
features), and Additivity (predictions as the sum of feature
contributions).
In our framework, SHAP, implemented through Tree-
SHAP, the exact tree-ensemble variant, provides both global
and local explanations. TreeSHAP leverages the internal
structure of the XGBoost model to compute exact Shap-
ley values in time linear to the number of leaves, making
it far faster and more scalable than the model-agnostic
KernelSHAP alternative. The global explanation aggregates
feature contributions for a comprehensive view, offering
insights into the overall behavior of the AI model. This
is particularly important for understanding how the model
