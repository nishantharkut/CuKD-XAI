5 Conclusion
This paper introduces an efﬁcient and explainable XAI-based
IDS framework that mitigates the “black-box” limitations of
conventional AI-IDS by integrating statistical feature selec-
tion (ANOVA) with SHAP and LIME, thereby enhancing
interpretability while preserving high detection performance
and computational efﬁciency. The two-stage method not
only reduces the feature space by ≈70% but also enforces
consistency between SHAP and LIME local explanations,
validating the reliability of the interpretations. A comprehen-
sive evaluation on the CIC-DDoS2019, CICIoT2023, and 5G
PFCP datasets demonstrates robust attack-detection capa-
bility across diverse networking environments and attack
scenarios: the framework maintains an F1 Score ≥99% and
accelerates LIME explanation generation by up to 87 % with
the reduced feature set. Although a full head-to-head bench-
mark with the latest deep learning IDSs (e.g., DeepIDS and
SAIDD) is left for future work, their published detection
performance results are already matched or exceeded by our
lightweight XGBoost pipeline.
In future work, we will strengthen resilience to noisy
data by integrating noise-robust preprocessing (e.g., Isola-
tion Forest for outlier removal, Hampel and median ﬁltering
for bursty artefacts, and RobustScaler to temper heavy-tailed
feature distributions) and by injecting controlled Gaussian
and burst noise during training for data-level augmenta-
tion. To counter adversarial manipulation, we will extend
the training loop with Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM)
and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) adversarial examples,
followed by adversarial re-training and a lightweight detector
based on Mahalanobis distance to ﬂag perturbed ﬂows at run-
time. We also plan to develop a real-time online version and
explore federated deployment for privacy-preserving scala-
bility, while adding a packet-level module that leverages 1-D
CNN embeddings so that raw packet traces can be processed
alongside ﬂow features.
