Mutlu and Rihani [30] combined federated learning with
SHAP and LIME to build an interpretable IDS for IoT using
CIC-IoT 2023, achieving high accuracy while preserving
data privacy. Our work differs by extending beyond IoT to
legacy IP and 5G scenarios, and by adding a novel time-

While these studies advance XAI techniques for IDS, none

feature reduction pipeline, or quantify explanation genera-
tion speed, all three aspects that are central to the framework
proposed in this paper.
2.3 Efficiency enhancement of XAI techniques
The efﬁciency challenges of XAI techniques have been
acknowledged in prior research, given their importance for
real-time applications. Chuang et al. [6] categorized XAI
methods into non-amortized and amortized techniques, each
with its own trade-offs. Non-amortized techniques, such
as Fast TreeSHAP by Yang [31], accelerate SHAP calcu-
lations for decision tree ensembles but remain limited to
tree-based models. Lemaire et al. [32] introduced an efﬁcient
Shapley value algorithm tailored for Naive Bayes classiﬁers
and demonstrated its scalability. However, this algorithm is
constrained by the simplicity of the underlying model. Amor-
tized techniques, like that proposed by Santhiappan et al.
[33], enhance LIME’s efﬁciency using attention weights, but
they are primarily designed for text-based models, which
limits their applicability to IDS scenarios. Muschalik et al.
[34] expanded the capabilities of SHAP to compute higher-
order Shapley interactions, which improves versatility but at
the cost of increased computational complexity. In [35], the
authors applied a Random Forest model to the Edge-IIoT
dataset and employed SHAP and LIME for explainability,
without incorporating feature reduction, consistency checks,
or time-efﬁciency evaluation. Similarly, [36] used a Random
Forest model on the Application Layer DoS Attack Dataset
with only LIME explanations, also without feature reduction,
consistency checks, or time-efﬁciency evaluation. Despite
these advancements, none of these studies have thoroughly
investigated the efﬁciency of XAI techniques in the context
of network security. Efﬁciency challenges are also present in
5G/6G networking scenarios, where real-time explainabil-
ity is required for high-bitrate data ﬂows and large trafﬁc
volumes [37]. Tables 1 and 2 summarize the comparisons
