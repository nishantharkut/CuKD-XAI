Our framework includes an explanation consistency check
process to ensure the alignment of insights gained from
SHAP and LIME local explanations, which enhances the
reliability and trustworthiness of the model’s interpretations.
By comparing the explanations generated by both methods
on the same sample, we can identify consistencies and dis-
crepancies in feature importance, which helps to validate
the model’s decisions. This cross-veriﬁcation is particularly
valuable in scenarios where interpretability and accountabil-
ity are critical, such as in detecting complex attack patterns.
For example, in the case of a sample of a SYN Flood
attack, both SHAP and LIME consistently identiﬁed the
‘Number’ feature as the most inﬂuential factor driving
the prediction toward the malicious class. This consistency
underscores the robustness of the framework’s ability to
detect SYN Flood attacks with high conﬁdence. However,
discrepancies are also observed: For example, SHAP identi-
ﬁedthefeatures‘IAT’and ‘Header_Length’ascrucial
in pushing the prediction towards the malicious outcome,
whereas these features are less emphasized by LIME. Con-
versely, LIME, contrary to SHAP, emphasizes the feature
‘psh_flag_number’ as an important contributor. These
differences likely arise due to the inherent methodological
variations in how SHAP and LIME generate explanations.
SHAP considers all possible feature interactions in its game-
theoretic approach, while LIME relies on perturbations and
surrogate models to approximate feature inﬂuence locally.
Understanding these methodological differences can help to
reﬁne the feature selection process and to improve the model
interpretability further.
