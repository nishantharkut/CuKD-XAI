techniques, such as Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE),
Mutual Information (MI), or Wrapper Methods, ANOVA
does not rely on model-speciﬁc parameters and offers a
purely statistical perspective, making it a robust complement
to model-based interpretability tools like SHAP. By combin-
ing the outcomes of SHAP Global Explanation and ANOVA,
we achieve a more comprehensive selection of features,
integrating SHAP’s interpretability with ANOVA’s statisti-
cal precision. The Feature Reduction process merges these
results, assigning a 60% weight to SHAP Global Explanation
and 40% to ANOVA, prioritizing SHAP’s ability to capture
feature importance in the context of model predictions while
incorporating the statistical insights from ANOVA. Table 5
presents the top 30 features for the CICIoT2023 and CIC-
DDoS2019 datasets, and Table 6 lists the 9 features for the
5G PFCP datasets. These identiﬁed features align well with
the nature of each attack. For instance, in volumetric attacks,
which rely on generating a high rate of trafﬁc to overwhelm
the target, trafﬁc volume-related features such as ‘Number’
are among the most important. On the other hand, for the
Slowloris attack, ‘HTTP’ is a key feature, surpassed only by
the ‘Number’ feature, as this attack relies on slowly send-
ing incomplete HTTP requests to keep connections open.
