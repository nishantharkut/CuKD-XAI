To address these limitations, we propose a novel two-stage
XAI-based IDS framework that combines interpretability,
efﬁciency, and scalability, making it suitable for various
networkingenvironments.Ourapproachintroducestwoorig-

a dual-stage feature selection that fuses statistical rank-

achieving substantial dimensionality reduction without com-

paired with a time-efﬁciency evaluation of explanation

inefﬁciencies, addresses interpretability gaps, and adds a
robustness layer to the explanatory process.
In the ﬁrst stage, ANOVA [7] with SHAP Global Expla-
nation is applied to perform feature selection, thus reducing
feature complexity without sacriﬁcing detection accuracy.
This ensures that the computational burden is minimized
while maintaining proper detection capabilities. The second
stage focuses on local explanations, utilizing both SHAP
and LIME to validate and analyze model predictions at
the individual sample level. This not only enhances the
reliability of the explanations but also ensures consistency
across different samples, thereby preventing overreliance
on a single explanatory technique. To our knowledge, no
prior IDS framework combines these three elements—dual-
stage feature selection, SHAP–LIME cross-validation, and
time-efﬁciency evaluation—within a uniﬁed, reproducible
pipeline.
The contributions of this paper are threefold: First, we
introduce an efﬁcient and interpretable XAI-based IDS
framework tailored for legacy IP, IoT, and 5G networking
environments. Second, we demonstrate the signiﬁcant com-
putational efﬁciency gains, particularly in real-time explana-
tion generation, without compromising accuracy. Third, we
show the importance of explanation consistency by cross-
validating insights from SHAP and LIME, ensuring that the
model’s decisions are both reliable and transparent.
This article extends and signiﬁcantly improves upon our
earlier conference paper [8]. Compared to the original ver-
sion, this manuscript introduces three main enhancements
along with several methodological improvements:
1. A new dataset family that includes 5G PFCP Session
Establishment and Session Deletion attacks, showing that
the framework generalizes beyond legacy IP and IoT traf-
ﬁc;
2. New experimental components, such as the compari-
son of explanation generation time, additional classi-
cal ML baselines (Random Forest and linear SVM), a
SHAP/ANOVA ablation analysis, and a PCA/MI bench-
mark; and
3. An extended efﬁciency evaluation that measures SHAP
and LIME explanation time across different feature sets
and hardware, and includes full hyperparameter settings,
dataset statistics, and public code for reproducibility.
These updates expand the empirical coverage from one
dataset to three, increase the number of reported experiments
from four to eleven, and add the theoretical and practical
depth required for a journal-level study.

2 Related work
2.1 Feature selection methods
Recent research has explored various feature selection
strategies for improving IDS performance. Ferreira et al.
[9] benchmarked multiple classiﬁers across UNSW-NB15,
NSL-KDD, and CIC-IDS2017, showing that XGBoost with
Lasso or ANOVA feature selection provides strong accu-
racy but leaving the interpretability problem largely unad-
dressed. Khani et al. [10] extended this direction by applying
XAI-based selectors (SHAP, GradCAM, Guided Backprop-
agation) alongside classical ANOVA, RFE, and Decision
Tree methods for encrypted trafﬁc, demonstrating that
SHAP often rivals or surpasses conventional approaches,
yet their study remained focused on CNN-based classi-
ﬁcation and feature ranking. Shaker et al. [11] further
leveraged SHAP within a wrapper-based scheme for early-
stage APT detection, reducing 77 features to four with
