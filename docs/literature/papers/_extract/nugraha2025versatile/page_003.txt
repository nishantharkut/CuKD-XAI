2.2 Application of XAI in intrusion detection systems
The advancements in AI, particularly in ML and DL, have
signiﬁcantly improved IDS capabilities for detecting Dis-
tributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks by analyzing
large-scale data to identify anomalies [13]. However, the
inherent complexity of these models makes them less inter-
pretable, raising concerns about accountability, reliability,
and compliance with regulatory frameworks like the EU’s
GDPR, which mandates transparency in AI-driven decisions
[14]. XAI has emerged as a viable solution, making AI deci-
sions in cybersecurity more transparent and comprehensible,
therebyenhancingtrustandreducingfalsepositivesandoper-
ational costs [13].
Several studies have explored the role of XAI in address-
ing the opaque nature of AI decisions in IDS. A recent survey
by Khan et al. [15] provides a broad overview of XAI-based
IDS in Industry 5.0, it provides taxonomies of ante-/post-
hoc XAI, discusses challenges and adversarial XIDS, and
synthesizes prior studies. Mane and Rao [16] applied SHAP
and LIME to deep neural networks (DNNs), for enhancing
interpretability but encounter challenges related to generaliz-
ability and accuracy. Mahbooba et al. [17] employed decision
trees to simplify interpretability but faced issues with overﬁt-
ting and the increasing sophistication of cyber-attacks. Wang
et al. [18] integrated CNN and LSTM with SHAP and LIME
for trafﬁc analysis but struggled with the computational
demands of real-time detection. Abou El Houda et al. [19]
observed that many studies lack detailed analyses correlating
features with speciﬁc attack types, which limits their prac-
tical applicability. Barnard et al. [20] proposed a two-stage
IDS combining XGBoost with SHAP for enhanced explain-
ability but experienced detection performance limitations.
Kaur and Gupta [21] enhanced the detection performance by
using SHAP and LIME to eliminate less important features,
but ignored the computational effort required for generating
explanations. Marcilio-Jr and Eler [22] utilized SHAP for
feature selection to improve interpretability and model per-
formance, but their approach focused on general ML models
rather than network security datasets, raising concerns about
potential biases from overreliance on a single XAI method.
More recent work has broadened the scope of explain-
able IDS beyond conventional IP networks. Khan et al.
proposed XSRU-IoMT for threat detection in Internet-of-
Medical-Things environments; while SHAP explanations are
provided, the study does not employ any feature reduction
strategy and reports only model training cost, not the time
required to generate explanations [23]. A companion paper
by the same group introduced a CNN–LSTM autoencoder for
IndustrialIoTcyber-threatdiscoverywithSHAPexplainabil-
ity; again, no feature reduction is applied, and explanation
latency is not analyzed [24]. Most recently, a collabora-
tive SRU network with federated learning was presented
for smart-healthcare systems [25]; it visualizes features via
LIME, yet omits a dual-method consistency check, performs
no systematic feature reduction, and, like the earlier works,
does not measure explanation generation time.
Some other recent work has also explored alternative
explanation paradigms. Burkart and Huber proposed an
Explanation Framework for Intrusion Detection based on
counterfactual examples [26]; although it yields human-
readable “what-if” scenarios, the study is conﬁned to NSL-
KDD, applies no feature reduction step, and does not report
explanation generation latency. Cumi´c et al. generated coun-
terfactualexplanationsforarandom-forestmodelthatdetects
SQL-Injection attacks [27]; again, no dimensionality reduc-
tion or timing analysis is provided, and the method is
evaluated on a single SQL dataset rather than 5G/6G trafﬁc.
Ables et al. extracted eclectic rule sets from a deep neural-
network IDS to create a surrogate decision tree [28]; while
the rules improve interpretability, the paper does not address
real-time efﬁciency, nor does it employ feature selection or
consider emerging 5G control-plane datasets.
Complementary XAI directions have also been investi-
gated. Burkart and Huber proposed a counterfactual-based
Explanation Framework for Intrusion Detection, but it is
evaluated only on KDD-based datasets, applies no feature
reduction step, and does not report explanation latency or
consider 5G trafﬁc [26]. Cumi-Guzman et al. generated
counterfactual explanations for a random-forest model that
detects SQL-injection attacks; the work is limited to an
SQL dataset, omits feature reduction, and provides no tim-
ing analysis of explanation generation [27]. Ables et al.
extracted eclectic rule sets from a DNN-based IDS and dis-
cuss rule-extraction speed, yet the study does not measure
per-instance explanation time, performs no feature reduc-
tion, and is conﬁned to UNSW-NB15 and CIC-IDS-2017
datasets without any 5G/6G scenarios [28]. Ouhssini et al.
[29] developed a CNN–LSTM model for DDoS defense
in cloud environments, integrating SHAP to identify the
ten most inﬂuential features across CIC datasets. Unlike
