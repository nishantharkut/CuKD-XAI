datasets. In particular, on WSN-DS, RFC demonstrated high accuracy and robustness, yielding an accuracy of 
99.94%, precision of 99.94%, and recall of 99.94%. Similarly, on the TON-IoT dataset, RFC also showed strong

well in these specific scenarios, its generalizability to other intrusion detection tasks with varying attack profiles 
will depend on how closely the features and data distributions in new datasets match those of WSN-DS and 
TON-IoT. Nonetheless, given RFC’s robustness in these cases, we are confident that it could offer competitive 
performance in a variety of contexts.
Our methodology enhances the scalability and robustness of IDS in resource-constrained environments 
like WSNs by integrating an adaptive framework that leverages StandardScaler, KMS for data balancing, PCA 
with Kaiser Criterion for dimension reduction, and RFC. StandardScaler ensures feature standardization, which 
stabilizes the model and improves performance. KMeans SMOTE addresses class imbalance by generating 
synthetic samples of minority classes, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to detect underrepresented attack 
patterns. PCA, applied using the Kaiser Criterion, reduces the dimensionality of the data while retaining only the 
most significant features, significantly lowering computational complexity. Finally, the RFC classifier is employed 
to effectively classify attacks with high accuracy and resilience, even in environments with limited resources. We 
validated the proposed approach on the WSN-DS and TON-IoT datasets, demonstrating its robust performance 
across diverse network conditions and attack profiles. This adaptive methodology ensures our model’s scalability, 
efficiency, and resilience, making it well-suited for deployment in WSNs.
While advanced DL architectures, such as hybrid models, have demonstrated strong performance in domains 
like power systems (Graph wavelet CNN)34 and in network attack detection (CNN+RNN)25, we find that 
ML models are more compatible and effective in enhancing adaptability to complex attack patterns in WSNs 
than DL. DL models often require substantial computational resources, which may not be optimal in WSN 
environments with limited resources. Therefore, we focus on the use of the ML model, which offers a more 
efficient and effective solution for intrusion detection in such sensors and IOT netowrks.
