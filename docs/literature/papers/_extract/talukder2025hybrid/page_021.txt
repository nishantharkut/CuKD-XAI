techniques, such as GAN-based oversampling and STL methods, to compare their effectiveness (Table 6). Our 
results demonstrate that KMS provides superior adaptability to diverse attack types by effectively balancing class 
distributions while maintaining computational efficiency (Table 15). Additionally, the integration of PCA with 
the Kaiser Criterion ensures dimensionality reduction tailored to the network’s topology, further enhancing 
the robustness of our intrusion detection system (Fig. 2). This combination makes our framework versatile and 
effective in adapting to the dynamic nature of WSNs.
Addressing the continuous learning and adaptation to emerging threats in WSNs
Our research addresses the crucial aspect of continuous learning and adaptation by designing a flexible and 
robust intrusion detection framework tailored to the dynamic nature of WSNs. The integration of KMS ensures 
adaptability to evolving attack patterns by effectively addressing class imbalances, thereby enhancing the model’s 
capacity to detect underrepresented threats. PCA with the Kaiser Criterion enables dynamic dimensionality 
reduction, focusing on the most critical features as data characteristics change, which optimizes computational 
efficiency. Additionally, the use of RFC provides high accuracy and resilience across diverse network conditions 
and attack profiles. By validating the methodology on benchmark datasets like WSN-DS and TON-IoT, we 
demonstrate its ability to adapt to emerging threats and varying scenarios, ensuring its scalability and practical 
applicability in real-world WSN environments.
Conclusion
In this research, we proposed a novel intrusion detection system (IDS) for wireless sensor networks (WSNs) by 
integrating K-means Smote (KMS) with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Random Forest Classifier 
(RFC). Our approach aimed at improving the performance and efficiency of IDS by addressing the challenges of 
class imbalance and dimensionality reduction in real-time applications. The experimental results demonstrated 
that our proposed model outperforms existing state-of-the-art models, achieving higher accuracy, precision, 
recall, and f1-score across different datasets, including WSN-DS and TON-IoT Network. The comparative 
analysis showed that KMS+PCA+RFC delivers superior classification performance, with the ability to effectively 
handle large-scale datasets and imbalanced classes. Moreover, the complexity analysis revealed that our model 
strikes a balance between classification accuracy and computational efficiency, making it suitable for deployment 
in resource-constrained environments such as WSNs. The time efficiency of our model was competitive, 
showing minimal increases in training and prediction times compared to other models. Overall, our research 
contributes to advancing IDS in WSNs, providing a robust and scalable solution for intrusion detection in IoT-
based networks, and offering valuable insights for future work in this domain, particularly in optimizing the 
trade-off between model complexity and real-time application performance.
Despite the promising results of our proposed model, there are certain limitations that need to be addressed. 
First, the performance of the model relies on the quality of the feature extraction techniques, which may vary 
across different network conditions and types of attacks. Second, our approach does not yet account for the 
dynamic nature of wireless sensor networks, where environmental factors and changing network conditions 
could affect the model’s privacy and accuracy.
For future work, we aim to explore adaptive feature selection methods that can dynamically adjust based on 
real-time network conditions. Furthermore, we plan to investigate federated learning techniques to improve the 
privacy of our model in large-scale, distributed WSN environments.
