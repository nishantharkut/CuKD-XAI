A hybrid machine learning model 
for intrusion detection in wireless 
sensor networks leveraging data 
balancing and dimensionality 
reduction
Md. Alamin Talukder1, Majdi Khalid2 & Nasrin Sultana3
Intrusion detection systems are essential for securing wireless sensor networks (WSNs) and Internet of 
Things (IoT) environments against various threats. This study presents a novel hybrid machine learning 
(ML) model that integrates KMeans-SMOTE (KMS) for data balancing and principal component analysis 
(PCA) for dimensionality reduction, evaluated using the WSN-DS and TON-IoT datasets. The model 
employs classifiers such as Decision Tree Classifier, Random Forest Classifier (RFC), and gradient 
boosting techniques like XGBoost (XGBC) to enhance detection accuracy and efficiency. The proposed 
hybrid (KMS + PCA + RFC) approach achieves remarkable performance, with an accuracy of 99.94% and 
an f1-score of 99.94% on the WSN-DS dataset. For the TON-IoT dataset, it achieves 99.97% accuracy 
and an f1-score of 99.97%, outperforming traditional SMOTE TomekLink and Generative Adversarial 
Network-based data balancing techniques. This hybrid approach addresses class imbalance and high-
dimensionality challenges, providing scalable and robust intrusion detection. Complexity analysis 
reveals that the proposed model reduces training and prediction times, making it suitable for real-time 
applications.
Keywords  Intrusion detection systems, Wireless sensor networks, Internet of Things, Hybrid machine 
learning, Model, Dimensionality reduction, Data balancing techniques
Internet of Things (IoT) networks and wireless sensor networks (WSNs) have become pivotal in modern 
technology, playing a crucial role in numerous fields, including industrial automation, smart cities, agriculture 
and healthcare1. WSNs consist of spatially distributed autonomous sensors that monitor and record 
environmental conditions, while IoT networks enable interconnected devices to communicate and exchange 
data seamlessly2. Despite their potential, these networks are vulnerable to numerous security threats, including 
data tampering, denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, routing attacks, and eavesdropping, which can severely 
compromise their performance and reliability3,4. The dynamic and resource-constrained nature of WSNs and 
IoT networks presents unique security challenges5. Traditional methods for intrusion detection often fall short 
due to their inability to adapt to the heterogeneous and evolving attack patterns in these networks6,7. Over the 
years, several machine learning (ML)-based approaches have been proposed for intrusion detection systems 
(IDS) in WSNs and IoT networks8,9. These approaches leverage the capability of ML to learn complex patterns, 
enabling automated detection of anomalies and malicious activities with improved accuracy compared to rule-
based systems10,11.
ML has emerged as a powerful tool in IDS due to its ability to automatically identify complex patterns and 
anomalies in network data12,13. ML models can effectively detect known and unknown attacks by learning from 
historical data, offering dynamic and adaptive solutions14–17. Techniques like Decision Trees (DT), Random 
Forests (RF), and Gradient Boosting classifiers provide high accuracy, making them ideal for real-time threat 
detection in WSN and IoT environments18.
