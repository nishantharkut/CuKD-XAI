Abinayaa et al.24 introduced an innovative approach to intrusion detection in WSN by combining the CatBoost 
classifier (Cb-C) with the Lyrebird Optimization Algorithm (LOA). Cb-C effectively handles imbalanced datasets 
commonly found in ID settings. Inspired by the Lyrebird’s ability to mimic environmental sounds, the LOA is a 
metaheuristic optimization algorithm. The proposed method was evaluated using the WSN-DS dataset. Among 
the models tested, LOA-Cb-C achieved the highest accuracy of 99.66% and 99% of precision, recall and f1-score 
and the lowest error rate of 0.34%, significantly improving WSN-IoT security by enhancing detection accuracy 
and reducing false alarms.
Salmi et al.25 developed an effective IDS to address intelligent and frequent attacks in WSNs, particularly 
Denial of Service (DoS) attacks. Traditional IDS methods were deemed less effective against these complex 
threats. The study reviewed works focused on detecting DoS attacks in WSNs and implemented several DL-
based IDS. These systems were trained on the WSN-DS dataset to detect four types of DoS attacks: Blackhole, 
Grayhole, Flooding, and Scheduling. The CNN+RNN model achieved significant performance with 85.19% 
precision, 85.29% recall, 82.34% f1-score, and 96.86% accuracy.
Dontu et al.26 developed a multi-criteria framework to strengthen WSN cybersecurity, utilizing DL algorithms 
to prioritize threats according to their severity and context. The study introduced the Enhanced Adaptive Butterfly 
Optimization Algorithm (EABOA) for feature selection, addressing challenges such as slow convergence, 
vulnerability to local optima, and lack of population diversity. Additionally, a new adaptive fragrance model was 
proposed to enhance algorithm performance, incorporating Lévy flight for better exploration and escape from 
local optima. The experimental results showed that the model on WSN-DS achieved an accuracy of 94.76%, 
precision of 86%, recall of 80%, and an F-score of 80.06%.
Vaishnavi et al.27 conducted a study on detecting Denial of Service (DoS) attacks in WSNs using ML techniques. 
The research addresses the challenges of DoS attacks in WSN environments, using the WSN-DS dataset for 
analysis. It applies data preprocessing methods like PCA and Min-Max Scaling and employs Convolutional 
Neural Networks (CNN) and XGBoost algorithms in various combinations. Among these models, XGBoost 
with Min-Max Scaling achieved the highest accuracy of 99.70%, outperforming the other approaches.
Related works on TON-IoT-DS
Cao et al.28 developed an innovative approach to simulate complex IoT scenarios by integrating multiple datasets. 
This method employed sine and cosine cyclic encoding for temporal attributes and addressed data imbalance 
using (STL) SMOTE for oversampling and an improved Tomek link removal for under-sampling. ML algorithms 
were utilized to classify data, distinguishing between normal and attack instances. Multiclass classification 
identified specific attack types, enriching the attack database, while normal data was stored securely for future 
use. Evaluation demonstrated exceptional performance, achieving 98.87%, 99.36%, 99.18%, and 99.18% for 
accurac, precision, recall, and F1 score respectively.
Uddin et al.21 developed a semi-supervised learning-based IDS that does not require labeled attack data. 
The approach consisted of two methods: (1) training a supervised model using randomly generated synthetic 
attack samples, and (2) creating a One-Class Classification (OCC) model using only benign network traffic. Both 
methods were tested on benchmark IDS datasets. The proposed usfAD model achieved 99.43% accuracy, 98.39% 
precision, and 99.37% f1-score on the TON-IoT Network dataset. Results demonstrated that the OCC model, 
utilizing the usfAD anomaly detection technique, outperformed conventional supervised and other OCC-based 
methods, particularly in detecting new, unseen attacks.
Hajla et al.29 proposed an improved intrusion detection system (IDS) framework to strengthen cybersecurity 
in IoT networks. Departing from traditional IDS methods that relied on predefined attack patterns, this 
framework focused on multiclass attack categorization using the TON-IoT dataset. It employed STL to balance 
the dataset and ensemble techniques such as voting and stacking, which integrated decision trees, resilient 
backpropagation multilayer perceptrons (RProp MLPs), and logistic regression to enhance accuracy and reduce 
errors. The ensemble voting model outperformed individual classifiers, achieving 96.32% accuracy, 93.11% 
precision, 84.55% recall, and 88.63% f1-score, demonstrating its effectiveness in detecting IoT cyber threats.
Li et al.30 analyzed feature extraction and selection techniques for IoT network intrusion detection using the 
TON-IoT dataset. Feature extraction demonstrated superior detection accuracy, greater feature retention, and 
stability with varying feature counts, while feature selection excelled in reducing model training and inference 
time. Decision trees (DT), using PCA with 33 features, achieved 77.62% accuracy, 67.34% precision, 48.67% 
recall, and a 45.53% f1-score. The research highlighted the strengths and limitations of each approach, offering 
insights into selecting effective methods for IoT intrusion detection and addressing prior gaps in the field.
Torre et al.19 developed a federated learning (FL) IDS utilizing a 1D CNN to enhance IoT network security. 
To address privacy concerns in FL, the system incorporated Differential Privacy, Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange, 
and Homomorphic Encryption. The approach was evaluated on the TON-IoT dataset, achieving exceptional 
results with an accuracy of 99.63%, precision of 96.48%, recall of 93.18%, and an f1-score of 94.45%. These 
outcomes demonstrated the model’s effectiveness in accurately detecting intrusions within IoT networks while 
maintaining data privacy.
Uddin et al.22 introduced a two-tier, one-class classification-based IDS. The first tier differentiates between 
normal behavior and attacks, while the second tier identifies known and unknown attacks using a multi-
classification mechanism and clustering algorithm. This allows the system to recognize and adapt to new, unseen 
threats by clustering unknown attacks and retraining. By employing one-class classifiers (OCC), the model 
eliminates the need for attack samples, tackling issues like data imbalance and zero-day attacks. The proposed 
framework demonstrated strong performance, achieving an accuracy and f1-score of 98.94% on the TON-IoT 
network dataset, showing its practical deployment potential.
