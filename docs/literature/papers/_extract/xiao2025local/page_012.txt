been applied to open up a clear and rigorous explanation of

The CAM characterizes the extent of agreement between
each input feature vector and the output of the model’s pre-
dicted class. To be more precise, for each feature, the cosine
similarity is computed between its activation over the dataset
and the predicted probability vector, thus offering a contin-
uous relevance measure that indicates both the size and the
direction of the feature’s consistency with the model’s deci-
sion. This technique enables the detection of features that
are the main contributors to class discrimination, as well as
handling the correlations existing in the feature space. The
radar plot overlays two sensitivity proﬁles, S1 and ST, thus
enabling direct comparison of feature contributions under
different experimental setups. It is noteworthy that “Is CH”
emerges as the most inﬂuential feature, with sensitivity val-
ues exceeding 0.75 in the S1 proﬁle, indicating its decisive
role in distinguishing cluster head-related behavioral pat-
terns that are closest to attack detection. Besides, “Time,”
“Dist to CH,” and “Who CH” are among the leading features
that uncovered signiﬁcant contributions in overcoming the
limitations in topological and temporal dynamics in WSN
communications.
On the other hand, the parameters such as “DATA R,”
“DATA S,” and “Rank” have relatively low sensitivity val-
ues, based on which it can be suggested that these features
have a few but minor contributions in the decision space. The
departure between S1 and ST distributions implies that the
model’s sensitivity varies across different analytical settings,
indicating an interaction between feature relevance and the
adopted optimization or ensemble strategy.
Figure 7 represents the results of a quick sensitivity anal-
ysis, measuring the relative inﬂuence of each input feature
on the model’s output predictions. FAST sensitivity analysis
[29] is a perturbation-based technique aimed at making efﬁ-
cient estimations of the impact of features on the output of
the model. For each feature, its value is changed systemat-
ically within the observed range, and the model’s predicted
probabilities are checked for changes. After that, the sensi-
tivity scores are combined and normalized to allow a ranking
of feature importance to be drawn, which indicates those fea-
tures that best affect the model’s decisions. It uses a dual-axis
depiction that shows S1 (black) on the left scale and ST (blue)
on the right, giving a view of both absolute contribution and
normalizedimportanceat thesametime. Theresearchreveals
that “Is CH,” “Who CH,” and “Dist to CH” are the three fea-
tures with the highest sensitivity values in both S1 and ST,
and hence the most inﬂuential in changing the properties
of the classiﬁcation boundaries in wireless sensor network
intrusion detection. On the other hand, the “Data Sent to BS”
and “Expanded Energy” attributes, showing substantial dif-
ferences, pinpoint the consumption of energy together with
the ﬂow of data as the vital signs of the network’s abnormal
behavior. Moreover, the features “Rank” and “ADV S” have
such a small inﬂuence on other features that their role in the
network is expected to be that of a helper. The degree of simi-
larity between S1 and ST is the largest intersection of feature
trends in signiﬁcance from different perspectives of the anal-
ysis, which constitutes their strongest point of agreement.
