To ensure the effectiveness and reliability of the intrusion
detection system, the dataset was split into 80% training and
20% testing, while maintaining the original class distribution
using stratiﬁcation. Table 5 presents the number of samples
per class for both training and testing phases.
Table 6 visually summarizes the quantitative evaluation
of the base ensemble design (DNN + CAT; DNCA) and
its metaheuristically improved versions, DNCA + QIO
(DCQI) and DNCA + OOA (DCOA), by means of ﬁve
canonical performance indices: accuracy, precision, recall,
F1-score, and speciﬁcity, measured in both training and
testing regimes. The DNCA model deﬁnes the reference
framework, with an accuracy of 0.9218 (train) and 0.9217
(test), combined with high precision (0.9580/0.9557) and
speciﬁcity (0.9807/0.9804), which are indicative of a con-
servative decision boundary that suppresses false positive
rates effectively. However, the recall values (0.9218/0.9217)
indicatethepotentialforenhancementofsensitivitytominor-
ity or subtle attack classes. The addition of QIO (DCQI)
to DNCA was the most effective in raising the perfor-
mance level, reaching maximum training accuracy (0.9545)
and testing accuracy (0.9562), as well as a balanced and
raised F1-score (0.9593/0.9607), thus providing evidence
of the optimizer’s ability to adapt the decision surfaces in
high-dimensional feature manifolds correctly. The positive
predictive value increases signiﬁcantly when precision val-
ues are close to one (0.9700/0.9703), whereas speciﬁcity
metrics (0.9884/0.9892) reveal a stable performance under
noise. The DCOA variation also presented a signiﬁcant
improvement over the baseline, characterized by accuracies
of 0.9468 (train) and 0.9464 (test), with corresponding high
F1-scores (0.9530/0.9527). This implies the effectiveness of
OOA in improving generalization without overﬁtting, as evi-
denced by the negligible divergence between train and test
performance. It is worth noting that precision was always
higher than recall in all experiments, which points to a
deliberate preference for false alarm reduction being the
operationally most important feature in intrusion detection in
resource-limited wireless sensor networks. The achievement
ofhighaccuracy,balancedF1-scores,andsuperiorspeciﬁcity
in the metaheuristically augmented ensembles constitutes a
strong argument in favor of exploiting the synergy between
deep neural architectures, gradient-boosted decision trees,
and sophisticated population-based optimizers to generate
WSN security-context models that are stable, discriminative,
and generalizable.
