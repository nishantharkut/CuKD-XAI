Figure 4 visually compares the on-hand and forecasted
results via a column plot for the baseline ensemble model
(DNCA) and its metaheuristically optimized counterparts
(DCQI and DCOA) over different attack categories and
normal trafﬁc. The similarity between the measured and pre-
dicted bars allows a quick visual check of the respective
models’ potential to reﬂect the underlying class distributions.
When looking at the “Normal” class, all models demonstrate
that the measured and predicted values are very close, with
DCQI being the closest one, thus the best precision and recall.
On the other hand, the DNCA model is quite off in its predic-
tions for the “Flooding” and “TDMA” scenarios; both false
and true counts are signiﬁcantly underestimated. The opti-
mized models, in particular DCQI, extend the contributions
of these discrepancies to a great extent; hence, they are able
to improve decision boundary reﬁnement and balance detec-
tion. Moreover, DCOA also works towards closing the gap
between the on-hand and predicted counts, albeit to a lesser
extent than DCQI, which is parallel with its slightly lower
overall accuracy. Thus, the ﬁgure is a visual conﬁrmation of
the quantitative results, where the metaheuristic optimiza-
tion, particularly QIO, is shown to be the most effective in
achieving the predictive alignment with the actual network
behaviors in wireless sensor network intrusion detection.
