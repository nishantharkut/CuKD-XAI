Problem statement and research questions
Solving the problem of botnet detection in IoT environ-
ments is challenging due to several key factors. First, IoT
networks consist of highly diverse devices with varying pro-
cessing capabilities, operating systems, and communication
protocols, resulting in heterogeneous data that complicates
pattern recognition [12]. Second, the non-IID nature of IoT
data means that each device generates unique data distri-
butions, making it difﬁcult for traditional machine learning
models to generalize across the network. Additionally, cen-
tralized intrusion detection approaches are impractical for
IoT due to privacy concerns and bandwidth limitations, as
transferring massive amounts of data from devices to a cen-
tral server increases vulnerability to attacks and violates user
privacy. Finally, botnets evolve rapidly, adapting their attack
methods to avoid detection, which requires a ﬂexible, adap-
tive detection model capable of continuous learning without
compromising security [13, 14]. Together, these factors make
developing an effective, privacy-preserving, and adaptable
detection framework a complex yet critical task for IoT secu-
rity.
Based on the limitations identiﬁed in existing approaches,
the following research questions are posed to guide this study
and address critical gaps in IoT security frameworks.
• RQ1: How can federated learning be effectively utilized to
address the heterogeneity and non-IID nature of IoT data
while preserving privacy in botnet intrusion detection?
• RQ2: How does the integration of SHAP-based knowl-
edge distillation enhance model interpretability and ensure
alignment with globally relevant features in federated set-
tings?
• RQ3: What role does decentralized feature aggregation
play in improving the scalability and adaptability of botnet
detection frameworks across diverse IoT devices?
• RQ4: How does the proposed framework address the chal-
lenges posed by evolving botnet architectures and resource
constraints in IoT environments?
Contributions in this research
To address the unique challenges of IoT botnet detection, we
propose a novel privacy-preserving federated learning frame-
work that leverages SHAP-based knowledge distillation and
decentralized feature aggregation. In our approach, each IoT
device (client) independently trains a local model and com-
putes SHAP values to determine the importance of individual
features related to botnet activity, such as patterns indicative
of Mirai, Bashlite, or Torii infections. Instead of sharing raw
data, clients only transmit these SHAP-based feature impor-
tance scores, enabling the central server to aggregate feature
insights from a wide range of devices without compromising
data privacy. This aggregation forms a global feature proﬁle,
which identiﬁes and prioritizes the most relevant features for
intrusion detection across heterogeneous, non-IID IoT data.
The global model is then ﬁne-tuned with this distilled knowl-
edge, enabling it to adapt to diverse IoT environments and
detect botnet activities effectively. Our approach not only
preserves data privacy but also addresses data heterogeneity
and allows for a scalable solution that can continuously learn
from diverse, decentralized IoT networks.
Major contribution of this research is listed below:
• We propose a novel privacy-preserving federated learning
framework for IoT botnet intrusion detection, integrating
SHAP-based knowledge distillation to eliminate raw data
sharing and enhance model interpretability.
• The framework addresses IoT data heterogeneity and non-
IID challenges by aggregating feature importance scores
across decentralized clients, enabling robust and adaptable
botnet detection.
• Our approach improves global model accuracy and per-
formance while maintaining high detection effectiveness
across diverse IoT devices, leveraging decentralized fea-
ture aggregation to detect botnet-speciﬁc patterns while
preserving privacy.
• We propose a dynamic and adaptive learning mechanism
that allows the system to continuously incorporate new
feature insights from evolving IoT environments, ensuring
long-term robustness and adaptability to emerging bot-
net attack patterns without requiring full retraining of the
global model.
