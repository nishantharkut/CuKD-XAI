Metaheuristically optimized deep soft-voting ensemble
for explainable and resource-aware signal processing in wireless
sensor network intrusion detection
Xiongzhi Xiao1 · Wenzhou Duan1
Received: 14 August 2025 / Revised: 19 September 2025 / Accepted: 5 October 2025 / Published online: 3 November 2025
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag London Ltd., part of Springer Nature 2025
Abstract
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) are widely employed in various sensitive areas; however, due to their limited computing
power, energy, and memory, these networks are exposed to signal-level anomalies as well as cyber-attacks. This study aims
to introduce a metaheuristically optimized deep soft-voting ensemble that integrates Deep Neural Networks and CATBoost
classiﬁers to enable secure intrusion detection. To achieve a seamless integration between convergence accuracy and explo-
ration divergence, the authors have employed two complementary metaheuristics, Quadratic Interpolation Optimization (QIO)
and Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA), for tuning the hyperparameters and decision thresholds. The system derives 23
operational and topological signal features that represent normal trafﬁc and four major attack types, i.e., Blackhole, Grayhole,
Flooding, and TDMA Scheduling. Rigorous preprocessing, such as Variance Inﬂation Factor analysis, supports that the fea-
tures are independent and stable. The metrics used include accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, speciﬁcity, and AUC, with
the QIO-enabled model (DCQI) leading the way with the highest test accuracy of 95.62%.DCQI outperforms the baseline
(DNCA) and the OOA-enhanced models, and thus, both class-level detection and balanced performance can be achieved. The
feature sensitivity analysis based on the Cosine Amplitude Method and ranking both distinctly and consistently indicates Is
CH, Who CH, and Dist to CH as the most inﬂuential predictors, which not only strengthens the understanding of WSNs but
also shows the computational efﬁciency of the adopted security.

1 Introduction
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) have emerged as one
of the most transformative paradigms in modern communi-
cation and computing systems. They are widely deployed
in diverse real-world applications, including environmen-
tal monitoring, industrial automation, disaster management,
healthcare, and military surveillance [1]. A typical WSN con-
sists of multiple energy-efﬁcient, cost-effective sensor nodes
that capture environmental changes, process the data locally,
and transmit it wirelessly to a sink node or base station
for further analysis. Depending on the deployment scenario,

These vulnerabilities make WSNs highly susceptible to

weaknesses by compromising nodes, eavesdropping on com-

threats, Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks are particularly
damaging, as they disrupt network functionality in multiple
ways.
Intrusion Detection Systems (IDSs) are therefore essential

known attacks but also adapt to previously unseen threats,

Several papers have been written on the implementation of
ML concepts to address the localization problem in wireless
sensor networks (WSNs). One of the energy-efﬁcient local-
ization techniques based on ML was the UWB frequency
spectrum combined with angle of arrival (AOA) [8] and
time difference of arrival (TDOA) [9] methods. The study by
Ahmadi et al. [10] highlighted that different sensor types with
speciﬁctechnicalfeaturescouldbeleveragedtoenhanceML-
based localization in WSNs. Although such methods provide
high accuracy, their heavy dependence on specialized hard-
ware increases deployment costs, motivating the search for
more affordable alternatives.
Multiple machine learning-based methods have been
proposed to address the limitations mentioned. For exam-
ple, Kim et al. [11] employed Support Vector Regression
(SVR) to design an RSSI-based localization algorithm that
improved robustness and precision. Similarly, a three-layer
feed-forward Artiﬁcial Neural Network (ANN) was reported
to achieve small error rates for indoor localization [12]. The
use of ANNs has been extensive. While an RSSI and KNN
proﬁling approach was characterized as having a low cost and
good estimation accuracy, a Bayesian-based plan for large-
scale Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) was introduced as
the ﬁrst in [13]. Merhi et al. [14] combined decision tree
(DT) classiﬁers with TDOA features to develop an acous-
tic target localization method. Furthermore, the Event-Based
MAC (EB-MAC) protocol for efﬁcient target acquisition in
acoustic WSNs was also their contribution.
Optimization methods have also been widely adopted to
improve localization further. For instance, Lee et al. [15]
integrated particle swarm optimization (PSO) with RSSI-
based models for indoor target tracking, while Wang et al.
[16] leveraged probability distributions of node distances
observed by anchor nodes. Cao and Xu [17] enhanced the
classical DV-Hop algorithm using optimum anchor node
subsets (OANS DV-Hop), employing both binary and contin-
uous PSO variants for improved precision. In another study,
TDOA and Kalman ﬁltering were combined with DV-Hop,
while ensemble optimization techniques further reduced bea-
con deployment errors and localization inaccuracies [18].
These approaches often achieved localization errors of less
than 2%, but they remain limited by computational demands
and challenges in large-scale deployments. Table 1 shows the
comparison of the recent articles with the present study.
This study builds on these directions and introduces
a metaheuristically optimized deep soft-voting ensemble
that combines Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) and CAT-
Boost classiﬁers to strengthen intrusion detection in WSNs.
The framework leverages two complementary metaheuris-
tic optimizers, Quadratic Interpolation Optimization (QIO)
and the Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA), to ﬁne-tune
hyperparameters and decision thresholds. A comprehensive
dataset of 23 operational and topological features, collected
under both normal conditions and diverse attack scenarios
(Blackhole, Grayhole, Flooding, and TDMA Scheduling), is
employed to validate the framework. The main contributions
of this work are as follows:
i.
Hybrid voting meta-ensemble architecture Develop-
ment of a soft-voting ensemble that integrates Deep
Neural Networks (DNNs) for nonlinear feature abstrac-
tion with CATBoost classiﬁers for effective handling of
tabular and categorical data. This combination leverages
the complementary strengths of deep representation
learning and gradient-boosted decision trees, resulting
in expressive yet stable decision boundaries.
ii.
Metaheuristic optimization for hyperparameter tuning
Incorporation of two complementary population-based
optimization algorithms, Quadratic Interpolation Opti-
mization (QIO) for rapid convergence and exploita-
tion, and Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA)
for exploratory search—ensuring robust hyperparam-
eter tuning, avoidance of premature convergence, and
improved decision thresholds.
iii. Feature-rich dataset design Compilation of a dataset
containing 23 network attributes, covering both nor-
mal trafﬁc and four major attack scenarios (Black-
hole, Grayhole, Flooding, and TDMA Scheduling).
Multicollinearity issues are addressed using the Vari-
ance Inﬂation Factor (VIF) analysis, thereby enhancing
model stability and interpretability.
iv.
Comprehensive evaluation metrics Performance assess-
ment across accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, speci-
ﬁcity, and AUC, with both aggregate and per-class
evaluations to ensure balanced performance across dif-
ferent attack types.
v.
Explainable sensitivity and feature importance analysis
Application of Cosine Amplitude Method (CAMs) and
sensitivity ranking to highlight key predictors such as Is
CH, Who CH, and Dist to CH. This enhances explain-
ability and strengthens trust in model decisions.
vi.

ensemble (DCQI) consistently outperforms both the

2 Data collection
To generate the dataset and gather the necessary data from the
transmitted and received packets within the WSN, a low-cost
monitoring service is required. On the other hand, all rele-
vant network data should be captured to aid in the detection,
classiﬁcation, and prevention of various potential assaults. In
this study, to disperse the load across sensor nodes, each sen-
sor participates in the monitoring process and must be able
to monitor a collection of its neighbors. The task was deter-
mining the optimal number of nodes to observe by a sensor
node in order to monitor all network sensors. The dataset was
gathered from a published study, which included an exper-
imental process for monitoring, with 374,661 samples, and
23 attributes were extracted to identify each node’s status in
the network [19]. The dataset is listed in Table 2.

3 Methods
3.1 Voting meta ensemble
The proposed Voting Meta-Ensemble combines the predic-
tive capabilities of multiple base learners to improve clas-
siﬁcation performance. Each independently trained model
produces a probability distribution over the target classes.
These outputs are then fused using soft voting, which aver-
ages the predicted class probabilities and selects the class
with the highest average. This approach allows models with
higher conﬁdence to exert greater inﬂuence on the ﬁnal deci-
sion, improving generalization compared to single models.
In this framework, DNNs extract complex nonlinear features,
while CATBoost efﬁciently handles tabular and categorical
data. The combination, optimized by metaheuristic algo-
rithms, mitigates both bias and variance, common limitations
of individual learners, resulting in robust and accurate intru-
sion detection [20, 21].
3.2 Deep neural networks
DNNs are employed for their ability to model complex non-
linear relationships in high-dimensional data. Each network
consists of an input layer, several hidden layers, and an
output layer. Hidden layers perform afﬁne transformations
followed by nonlinear activations, enabling hierarchical fea-
ture learning. A variable selection process ensures that only
informative features are used, reducing overﬁtting and com-
putational load. The DNN outputs class probabilities for
intrusion detection, trained via gradient-based optimization
and backpropagation [22, 23].

3.3 CAT-Boost classification (CATC)
CATBoost is a gradient-boosted decision tree algorithm opti-
mized for categorical and tabular data. It reduces overﬁtting
through ordered boosting and Bayesian estimation of feature
contributions.Inthisstudy,CATBoostoutputsclassprobabil-
ities that are used alongside DNN outputs in the soft-voting
ensemble. Equations detailing the internal computation of
prediction values have been simpliﬁed to focus on how fea-
tures are weighted and combined, rather than step-by-step
algebra, improving clarity while retaining technical accuracy
[24].
3.4 Metaheuristic optimization
Two population-based optimizers, Quadratic Interpolation
Optimization (QIO) [25] and the Osprey Optimization Algo-
rithm (OOA) [26, 27], are applied to ﬁne-tune hyperparam-
eters and decision thresholds.
• QIO emphasizes exploitation, allowing rapid conver-
gence by approximating optimal parameter values using
quadratic interpolation. Its fast convergence and local
search efﬁciency make it suitable for reﬁning decision
boundaries in high-dimensional feature spaces.
• OOA focuses on exploration, mimicking the hunting
behavior of ospreys to escape local optima and identify
globally optimal parameter conﬁgurations.
Compared to classical optimizers such as Particle Swarm
Optimization (PSO) or Genetic Algorithms (GA), QIO and
OOA provide a complementary balance of convergence
speed and exploration diversity. PSO often converges pre-
maturely, while GA requires extensive iterations and can be
computationally costly. By integrating QIO and OOA, the
ensemble achieves faster, more reliable optimization with
lower computational overhead.
3.5 Soft-voting ensemble operation
• DNN and CATBoost are independently trained on the fea-
ture set.
• Each model outputs a probability distribution over classes.
• Softvotingaveragestheseprobabilities,assigningtheclass
with the highest mean probability.
• Optimized hyperparameters from QIO and OOA improve
both model accuracy and ensemble reliability.
This design allows the ensemble to leverage the nonlin-
ear representation power of DNNs, the categorical handling
strength of CATBoost, and the efﬁcient hyperparameter
optimization of metaheuristics, yielding a robust intrusion
detection system for WSNs.

3.7 Model training and reproducibility
To ensure reproducibility and transparency, all models were
trained with explicit hyperparameter settings, data split strat-
egy, and preprocessing steps. The dataset was split into 80%
training and 20% testing, with stratiﬁcation applied to main-
tain the class distribution across splits. Table 4 shows the
hyperparameters and architectural details for reproducibil-
ity.
The DNN architecture consists of a single hidden layer
with 128 neurons, using ReLU activation for hidden lay-
ers and Sigmoid for output. Training was conducted with a
learning rate of 0.1, a batch size of 32, and 100 epochs. The
CATBoost classiﬁer was conﬁgured with a depth of 6, learn-
ing rate of 0.15, L2 regularization of 3, and the RMSE loss
function. The metaheuristic optimizers, QIO and OOA, were
set with a population size of 30, search bounds deﬁned per
hyperparameter, and an iteration limit of 200. The stopping
criterion was either convergence of the objective function or
reaching the maximum number of iterations.
4 Result and discussion
This section presents the results from the ensemble-based
intrusion detection framework, the proposal, and compares
its performance with that of baseline models. The presenta-
tion of results ﬁrst focuses on the convergence behavior of the
optimization-enhanced ensembles, and a subsequent stage
is the evaluation of their predictive performance by utiliz-
ing several classiﬁcation metrics. Besides the overall model
accuracy, the class-wise detection counts are also taken into
account, thus providing a deeper understanding of the inter-
actions between precision, recall, and F1-score for different
attack scenarios. The aspects of the model, such as stability,
behavior,andsensitivitytofeaturesthatresultinperformance
patterns being linked to the latent optimization strategies, are
revealed by convergence plots, heatmaps, ROC curves, and
sensitivity analyses.
Figure 2 depicts a radial convergence plot that provides a
visual comparison of the iterative performance trajectories of
DCQI and DCOA, two hybrid ensemble conﬁgurations, over
the entire process of optimization. The iteration progression
and accuracy convergence are evident in the radial layout,
which does not require detailed analysis, as each concentric
arc represents incremental increases in classiﬁcation accu-
racy. Right from the ﬁrst iterations, DCQI (in yellow) goes
to higher levels of accuracy faster, exceeding the value of
0.94 within the ﬁrst few cycles of the optimization process,
and also continuing with its upward trend until it reaches a
level above 0.955, where it stabilizes. This means that the
Quadratic Interpolation Optimization method is very effec-
tive in speeding up the convergence process by cutting off
the decision boundaries in a more targeted and exploitation-
drivenway.Conversely,DCOA(inlightred)tracesadifferent
pattern with a slower than DCQI convergence curve, where it
is only after a long number of iterations that it becomes com-
petitive at accuracy levels. Its ﬁnal performance is marginally
lower than that of DCQI, but the less steep slope signiﬁes that
OOA is more likely to employ the explorative search strategy,
which can be one of the reasons why it is less prone to prema-
ture convergence in more complex optimization landscapes.
The decrease of the performance gains in both models for the
last iterations is very gradual, which is a strong indication of
the saturation phase of the metaheuristic search, where the
near-optimal solutions have been reached and the marginal
improvements become smaller. The image as a whole high-
lights DCQI’s advantages, including fast convergence and
ﬁnal accuracy, while also showing DCOA as strong in the
exploration phase.

5 Conclusion
This study has unveiled and solidly substantiated a novel
intrusion detection framework for Wireless Sensor Networks
(WSNs), whereby the integration of artiﬁcial deep neural
networks, gradient-boosted decision trees, and metaheuris-
tic optimization strategies has been implemented to unlock
the latent capabilities of WSNs. Whereas the developed soft-
voting meta-ensemble, consisting of Deep Neural Networks
(DNNs) and CATBoost classiﬁers, had been thoroughly opti-
mized by applying Quadratic Interpolation Optimization
(QIO) and Osprey Optimization Algorithm (OOA), each sup-
plying a different set of exploration and exploitation aspects
to the indagation journey. The new arrangement is effectively
agnostic about the very hardware limitations, the nonsta-
tionarity of trafﬁc, and the variability of the opponent’s
tactic entailed by the WSN intrusion problem in resource-
limited environments. Extensive experimental assessments
include a multi-metric evaluation protocol (accuracy, pre-
cision, recall, F1-score, speciﬁcity, and AUC) as evidence
of the metaheuristically optimized ensembles outperforming
the baseline model. The QIO-enhanced ensemble (DCQI)
was notably the most performance-efﬁcient across all met-
rics, characterized by fast convergence, high sensitivity to
minority-class attack patterns, and great stability in the
decision boundaries. The OOA-boosted (DCOA), while not
outperforming DCQI in the last accuracy results, has proven
to be more of an exploratory search behavior, thus less-
ening the danger of premature convergence and providing
resilience in the more difﬁcult optimization scenarios. Sen-
sitivity of features to the model has been investigated by
means of the Cosine Amplitude Method (CAM) and FAST
feature ranking, not only to verify structural and topologi-
cal predictors, notably Is CH, Who CH, and Dist to CH as
the main driving factors, but also to suggest a stable pattern
of feature importance across optimization strategies. This
trust-enhancing factor opens up new opportunities for the
administrator’s intuitive problem-solving while also engag-
ing in the explainable AI debate community in cybersecurity
program/applicationﬁelds.Notably,theproposedframework
demonstrates robustness against unknown attacks and noisy
data by leveraging probabilistic soft-voting, metaheuristic
optimization, and feature sensitivity analysis. The ensemble
can identify abnormal patterns even when attack signatures
were not present in the training set, and its hierarchical fea-
ture representation allows resilience to random perturbations
or sensor noise.
5.1 Limitations
Even though the results of this research show potential,
the study still has some limitations that are worth mention-
ing. First of all, the dataset used, which is rich in features
and includes multiple high-impact attack types, was cre-
ated under the conditions of controlled experiments, so

the ability of the model to be generalized to large-scale,
diverse, and fully dynamic WSN deployments is still empiri-
cally conﬁrmed. Secondly, while metaheuristic optimization
may be less time-consuming than other methods, it still
requires ofﬂine training, which limits the possibility of real-
time retraining in highly volatile environments. Thirdly,
the range of attacks considered by the authors, although
including major threats such as Blackhole, Grayhole, Flood-
ing, and TDMA Scheduling, does not fully encompass the
undetectable and evolving intrusion patterns (for instance,
low-rate DoS, data exﬁltration, and coordinated attacks
across layers). Fourth, while the ensemble shows resilience
to noisy or partially missing data, extreme levels of sensor
failure or adversarial noise could still challenge detection
performance. Lastly, this research is based on the assumption
of the centralized detection model, while the distributed or
federated detection systems, which can be more suitable for
privacy-sensitive or geographically dispersed deployments,
were not part of the study’s focus.
5.2 Future directions
Due to these constraints, the researchers will concentrate on
broadening the design to include online and adaptive learning
paradigms. This will allow the system to change its decision
boundaries on the ﬂy as new and evolving attack vectors are
encountered. Moreover, the adoption of federated learning
might alleviate issues related to both scalability and privacy,
while the use of lightweight deep learning (e.g., quantized or
pruned networks) could make it easier for ultra-low-power
sensornodestobedeployed.Futureworkwillalsoinvestigate
enhanced anomaly detection strategies and noise-resilient
learning to improve the detection of completely novel attacks
and mitigate the impact of data corruption. Future works
will focus on multimodal intrusion detecting, integrating
cross-layer and contextual information that will improve the
system’s resistance to sophisticated and untraceable cyber-
attacks. To recap, this work offers a deep-ensemble-based
intrusion detection system optimized with metaheuristics
which is both computationally efﬁcient and interpretable.
Apart from being a potential avenue for the development
of next-generation security systems for wireless sensor net-
works that are not only robust, resource-aware, and adaptable
to the ever-increasing complexity and adversarial landscape
of wireless sensing environments, this framework also signi-
ﬁes such a fund.
