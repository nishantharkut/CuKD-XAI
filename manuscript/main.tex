\begin{abstract}
Wireless sensor network (WSN) intrusion detection under severe class imbalance demands detectors that fit kilobyte-scale memory and integer-arithmetic budgets while retaining accuracy on minority attack classes.
This paper presents \method{} (\emph{tiny RF knowledge distillation}), a controlled study of ultra-small multilayer-perceptron students distilled from a calibrated random-forest teacher on the \dataset{} corpus.
Under train-only scaling with ten seeds, RF-KD Student~A achieves macro-F1 $0.9203 \pm 0.0065$ at $1{,}189$ parameters ($4.64$\,KB) and Student~B achieves $0.9392 \pm 0.0129$ at $3{,}397$ parameters ($13.27$\,KB), representing $58.8\times$ and $20.6\times$ compression relative to the neural teacher.

A critical finding is that KD benefit is protocol-contingent: seed-paired RF-KD minus scratch yields $\Delta = +0.0094$ ($p = 0.048$) under random-row splits but only $+0.0002$ ($p = 0.95$) under feature-group splits, and per-route RNG isolation independently confirms the collapse ($\Delta = +0.0007$ for A).
Per-class analysis reveals minority-class tradeoffs: Student~B RF-KD loses $0.023$ Blackhole F1 relative to scratch while gaining Flooding and TDMA.

The fixed-point inference core cross-compiles for MSP430F1611-class motes in $2{,}842$\,B Flash.
Full-test hardware-in-the-loop ($N = 56{,}200$) on ESP32-C3 and Arduino UNO~R4 WiFi reproduces fixed-point references with agreement $1.0$ on all four student--board pairs, with float-to-fixed macro-F1 reduction $\approx 0.024$--$0.027$ and latencies from $116.5$\,$\mu$s to $791.4$\,$\mu$s.

Negative results are reported with the same rigor: global SHAP rank agreement is low and non-significant ($\rho \approx 0.24$, $p > 0.35$); co-distillation underperforms RF-KD by $0.0046$--$0.0069$ macro-F1; curriculum-extension collapses at seed-$5678$ under multi-route training.
Instrumented radio motes were unavailable, so commercial MCUs proxy the constrained on-node class via offline feature replay.
These findings support KB-scale executable neural detectors when evaluation protocols, split constructions, and negative results are reported alongside compression ratios.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} proliferation of wireless sensor networks (WSNs) for environmental monitoring, industrial sensing, and infrastructure surveillance has elevated on-node security analysis to a first-order systems problem~\cite{ghadi2024review}.
A practical detector must identify minority routing and traffic attacks from tabular features while its parameters, intermediate activations, and arithmetic fit devices with limited flash, RAM, and energy~\cite{banbury2021benchmarking,sze2017efficient}.
On the public \dataset{} corpus~\cite{almomani2016wsnds}, optimized ensembles and deep models report very high software accuracy~\cite{talukder2024mlstl,talukder2025hybrid,birahim2025pso,pandey2025tabu,nguyen2024gswo}.
These results establish a predictive ceiling but leave open a critical question: can a \emph{kilobyte-scale integer student} preserve task utility under an auditable training protocol and full-test hardware replay?

Knowledge distillation transfers soft targets from a teacher to a compact student~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
Stanton \emph{et al.} demonstrated that student--teacher fidelity and generalization need not move together~\cite{stanton2021kd}, framing KD as an empirical protocol question rather than a guaranteed improvement.
Recent intrusion detection system (IDS) studies apply KD to reduce neural size on classical and IoT traffic benchmarks~\cite{yang2023selfkd,wisanwanichthan2025kd,yagiz2025lens,benaddi2025lightweight,peng2025fdids,hossain2025federatedkd}.
In parallel, hardware-aware IDS targets gateway-class platforms such as Raspberry~Pi~\cite{diab2025hardware}, hybrid designs retain deep models at a gateway after on-node filtering~\cite{alfarra2025energy}, and tree detectors have been embedded on ESP32-class devices for smart-home traffic~\cite{javed2024thermostat}.

What remains under-specified is the joint case that matters for WSN routing attacks: RF-to-tiny neural compression on \dataset{}, under a train-only scaler and leakage-aware protocol ladder, with two evaluation units for multi-seed tables versus deploy export, two-board full-test integer agreement, and a quantitative post-KD explanation-rank diagnostic.
Explanation methods can disagree even when predictive metrics appear strong~\cite{krishna2022disagreement,adebayo2018sanity,lundberg2017shap}.
A responsible compression study must therefore report when distillation helps, when it does not, when integer conversion costs utility, and when global explanation ranks fail to transfer.

This paper studies that joint case under the name \method{}.
Curriculum ordering and co-distillation appear as controlled routes, not as guaranteed superiority claims.
Explainability enters as a measurement of global feature-rank transfer from RF TreeExplainer to student DeepExplainer, not as a claim that distillation preserves RF rankings.
We do not claim first KD for IDS, first MCU IDS, SHAP rank preservation, co-distillation superiority under train-only scaling, or that deploy seed-$42$ macro-F1 is a multi-seed table cell.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Primary multi-seed compression (\ums{}):} Fixed-capacity Students~A/B under train-only scaler and ten seeds, with RF-KD as the principal distilled route, reported against scratch, MLP-KD, co-distillation, curriculum, and control routes. Student~A achieves $58.8\times$ compression (4.64\,KB) with macro-F1 $0.9203 \pm 0.0065$; Student~B achieves $20.6\times$ compression (13.27\,KB) with macro-F1 $0.9392 \pm 0.0129$.

\item \textbf{Protocol sensitivity of KD benefit:} Seed-paired KD-minus-scratch contrasts random-row train-only against feature-group disjoint splits, revealing that KD gain for Student~A is significant ($p = 0.048$) under random-row but vanishes ($p = 0.95$) under feature-group; per-route RNG isolation independently confirms the collapse ($\Delta = +0.0007$ for A, $+0.0005$ for B), with per-class costs showing minority-class tradeoffs under the stricter protocol.

\item \textbf{Dual evaluation protocols:} Multi-seed tables (\ums{}) versus deploy protocol (\udc{}) for hardware export, demonstrating that the deploy seed-$42$ macro-F1 $0.9485$ is not interchangeable with multi-seed results.

\item \textbf{Deployment feasibility and dual-MCU full-test HIL:} Fixed-point inference core cross-compiles for MSP430F1611-class WSN motes in $2{,}842$\,B Flash with ${\sim}248$\,B stack, and full-test integer agreement on ESP32-C3 and Arduino UNO~R4 WiFi ($N = 56{,}200$) is $1.0$ on all four student--board pairs, with measured float-to-fixed utility cost $0.024$--$0.027$ macro-F1 and board latencies from $116.5$\,$\mu$s to $791.4$\,$\mu$s.

\item \textbf{Negative and boundary results:} Global SHAP rank transfer is low and non-significant ($\rho \approx 0.24$, $p > 0.35$); co-distillation underperforms RF-KD by $0.0046$--$0.0069$ macro-F1; curriculum-extension collapses at seed-$5678$ under multi-route training but recovers under isolated seeding; Edge-IIoTset group-aware evaluation shows lower absolute scores than standard random splits with identified $\approx 17\%$ test-row leakage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{WSN-DS and Software Detectors}
Almomani \emph{et al.} introduced \dataset{} from NS-2/LEACH simulations with five traffic classes: Blackhole, Grayhole, Flooding, TDMA, and Normal~\cite{almomani2016wsnds}.
Subsequent work emphasizes high software accuracy through resampling, feature selection, and ensemble methods~\cite{talukder2024mlstl,talukder2025hybrid,nguyen2024gswo,alqahtani2019gxgboost,birahim2025pso,pandey2025tabu,xiao2025ensemble,salmi2022cnnlstm}.
These papers establish a predictive ceiling against which ultra-small students should be judged but leave open whether kilobyte-scale neural students, under train-only scaling and two-board integer HIL, remain useful under explicit protocol controls.
Our work uses \dataset{} as a comparable public benchmark and explicitly reports the train-only scaler and fixed split protocol.

\subsection{Knowledge Distillation for IDS Compression}
Hinton \emph{et al.} formulated temperature-scaled soft-label distillation for model compression~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
Stanton \emph{et al.} showed that student--teacher fidelity and generalization need not move together, identifying optimization difficulties and dataset characteristics as key factors~\cite{stanton2021kd}.
Yang \emph{et al.} apply self-KD to lightweight network intrusion detection~\cite{yang2023selfkd}.
Wisanwanichthan and Thammawichai distill deep teachers into shallow networks across multiple IDS datasets on workstation hardware~\cite{wisanwanichthan2025kd}.
Yagiz and Goktas combine KD with variational autoencoders and attribution-based XAI~\cite{yagiz2025lens}.
Benaddi \emph{et al.} couple SHAP-guided feature pruning with Kronecker-compressed students on TON\_IoT~\cite{benaddi2025lightweight}.
Peng \emph{et al.} study federated learning with KD under Non-IID IoT traffic~\cite{peng2025fdids}.
Hossain \emph{et al.} use SHAP-based knowledge sharing in federated botnet detection~\cite{hossain2025federatedkd}.
Guo \emph{et al.} analyze why knowledge distillation works through attention and fidelity mechanisms~\cite{guo2024whykd}.
\method{} differs by centering RF soft targets into fixed ultra-small MLPs on \dataset{}, a train-only multi-seed protocol with a feature-group stress test, two evaluation units, and two-MCU full-test integer HIL.

\subsection{On-Device and Hardware-Aware IDS}
Jacob \emph{et al.} provide integer-arithmetic-only quantization foundations for neural network inference~\cite{jacob2018integer}.
Diab \emph{et al.} co-design tree and 1D-CNN detectors under flash/RAM/operation budgets and deploy on Raspberry~Pi~3, achieving $95.3\%$ accuracy with 75\,KB flash and $1.2$\,K operations for LightGBM, and $97.2\%$ accuracy with 190\,KB flash and $840$\,K FLOPs for a HW-NAS-optimized CNN~\cite{diab2025hardware}.
Alfarra and AbuSamra study energy-efficient hybrid WSN security with on-node rules and gateway CNN-LSTM~\cite{alfarra2025energy}.
Javed \emph{et al.} embed CatBoost on an ESP32 smart thermostat for DoS/MITM detection on a home-IoT feature set, achieving $98.71\%$ binary accuracy with $276$\,$\mu$s inference time~\cite{javed2024thermostat}.
Misrak \emph{et al.} study quantization-aware hybrid IDS models with software size reports~\cite{misrak2025quantization}.
Banbury \emph{et al.} and Sze \emph{et al.} survey TinyML and efficient DNN processing constraints~\cite{banbury2021benchmarking,sze2017efficient}.
Our HIL targets two MCU families as on-node proxies with full-test integer agreement for RF-distilled neural students, not gateway-class training/inference and not live radio energy accounting.
We report inference latencies from $116.5$\,$\mu$s to $791.4$\,$\mu$s across four student--board pairs with full-test agreement $1.0$.

\subsection{Explainability and Disagreement}
Lundberg and Lee introduced SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for model interpretation~\cite{lundberg2017shap}.
Krishna \emph{et al.} document systematic disagreement among post-hoc explainers even when predictive metrics are strong~\cite{krishna2022disagreement}.
Adebayo \emph{et al.} provide sanity checks for saliency methods~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}.
Nugraha \emph{et al.} integrate SHAP for IDS feature workflows~\cite{nugraha2025versatile}.
We measure global rank Spearman correlation between RF TreeExplainer and student DeepExplainer on the deploy unit and report low non-significant agreement ($\rho \approx 0.24$, $p > 0.35$) as a measured failure of global rank transfer, consistent with explanation disagreement literature.

\section{System Model and Evaluation Boundary}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Conceptual Placement}
Fig.~\ref{fig:system} distinguishes three layers of the problem.
In a fielded WSN, sensing and short-range radio terminate at a sink or gateway, with optional cloud services beyond.
An intrusion detector may run \emph{on-node} (severe resource limits), at the \emph{gateway} (SBC-class resources), or remotely.
Hybrid designs intentionally leave deep models at the gateway~\cite{alfarra2025energy}.
Gateway-class hardware-aware search targets Pi-class platforms~\cite{diab2025hardware}.
\method{} targets the on-node class: ultra-small students intended for constrained microcontrollers.
The evaluation boundary is explicit: we measure model-core fixed-point fidelity and latency on commercial MCUs via offline feature replay, not over-the-air LEACH dynamics or joules-per-packet on a live mesh.

\subsection{Dataset and Threat Setting}
\dataset{} provides a public five-class WSN routing/DoS setting with a large software literature, enabling protocol comparisons that a private mote capture would not support.
Class counts in our repository CSV are $10{,}049$ Blackhole, $3{,}312$ Flooding, $14{,}596$ Grayhole, $340{,}066$ Normal, and $6{,}638$ TDMA ($374{,}661$ rows, $17$ numeric features after removing identifier and label).

\section{Proposed \method{} Framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote the WSN-DS dataset with features $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ ($d=17$) and labels $y_i \in \{1, \ldots, C\}$ ($C=5$ classes).
Given a fixed stratified split into training $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}$, validation $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}$, and test $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$ partitions, our objective is to train a compact neural network $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{C}$ parameterized by $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{|\theta|}$ that minimizes the test macro-F1 while satisfying a memory constraint:
\begin{equation}
\min_\theta \; \mathcal{M}(f_\theta; \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad |\theta| \leq \Theta_{\max},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}$ denotes macro-F1 and $\Theta_{\max}$ represents the parameter budget.
We define the \emph{compression ratio} relative to the neural teacher $f_{\theta_{\mathrm{teacher}}}$ as:
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{|\theta_{\mathrm{teacher}}|}{|\theta_{\mathrm{student}}|}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Teachers and Students}
\label{sec:architectures}
The RF teacher uses $500$ trees and maximum depth $15$~\cite{breiman2001random}.
For distillation, class probabilities are isotonic-calibrated with three-fold cross-validation~\cite{guo2017calibration}.
A neural teacher MLP ($17$--$128$--$256$--$128$--$5$; $69{,}893$ parameters; $273.02$\,KB FP32 parameter payload) provides an optional soft-target baseline.
Student~A is $17$--$32$--$16$--$5$ ($1{,}189$ parameters, $1{,}136$ MACs, $4.64$\,KB FP32).
Student~B is $17$--$64$--$32$--$5$ ($3{,}397$ parameters, $3{,}296$ MACs, $13.27$\,KB FP32).
Architectures are fixed across routes; routes change losses and teachers, not capacity.

\subsection{Training Routes and Losses}
\label{sec:routes}
Let $z = f_\theta(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{C}$ denote the logits and $\sigma(z)_c = \exp(z_c) / \sum_{j=1}^{C} \exp(z_j)$ the softmax output.
For knowledge distillation, we use temperature-scaled softmax:
\begin{equation}
\sigma^{(T)}(z)_c = \frac{\exp(z_c / T)}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} \exp(z_j / T)},
\end{equation}
where $T > 0$ controls the smoothness of the distribution.
As $T \rightarrow \infty$, the distribution approaches uniform; as $T \rightarrow 0$, it approaches a one-hot distribution.

The scratch objective uses class-weighted cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\theta) = -\sum_{c=1}^{C} w_c\, y_c \log \sigma(f_\theta(x))_c,
\end{equation}
where $w_c$ are inverse-frequency class weights.
For RF-KD, we combine hard and soft supervision as a convex balance:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RF\text{-}KD}}(\theta) &= (1 - \alpha)\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\theta) \\
&\quad + \alpha\, T^2\, \mathrm{KL}\!\left(q^{\mathrm{RF}} \| \sigma^{(T)}\right),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ balances hard and soft supervision.
The temperature $T=4$ and $\alpha=0.7$ are used for all primary experiments, selected by grid search ($T \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$, $\alpha \in \{0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$) against leakage-free validation.
The KL divergence term encourages the student to match the teacher's predictive distribution:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}(p \| q) = \sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c \log \frac{p_c}{q_c}.
\end{equation}
Co-distillation (route J) applies joint soft supervision from the RF teacher and a curriculum-trained MLP~\cite{benaddi2025lightweight}:
{\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{J}}(\theta) &= w_{\mathrm{CE}}\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} + w_{\mathrm{RF}}\, T^2 \mathrm{KL}(q^{\mathrm{RF}} \| \sigma^{(T)}) \\
&\quad + w_{\mathrm{CL}}\, T^2 \mathrm{KL}(q^{\mathrm{CL}} \| \sigma^{(T)}),
\end{split}
\end{equation}}
with $w_{\mathrm{CE}} = 0.30$, $w_{\mathrm{RF}} = 0.40$, $w_{\mathrm{CL}} = 0.30$, where $q^{\mathrm{CL}}$ denotes soft targets from the curriculum-trained MLP.

\subsection{Fidelity and Agreement Metrics}
\label{sec:fidelity}
Following Stanton \emph{et al.}~\cite{stanton2021kd}, we distinguish between \emph{fidelity} (agreement with the teacher) and \emph{generalization} (test performance).
We define the agreement metric between a student $f_\theta$ and reference $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on test set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$:
{\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Agree}(f_\theta, f_{\mathrm{ref}}) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\hat{y}_\theta(x_i) = \hat{y}_{\mathrm{ref}}(x_i)\right],
\end{equation}}
where $N = |\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}|$ and $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function.
For MCU HIL, the reference $f_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the host fixed-point model.
We also report the predictive KL divergence:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}_{\mathrm{avg}}(f_\theta, f_{\mathrm{ref}}) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\sigma(f_{\mathrm{ref}}(x_i)) \| \sigma(f_\theta(x_i))\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Train-Only Scaler}
\label{sec:train-only}
After the fixed seed-$42$ stratified $70$/$15$/$15$ split, a \texttt{StandardScaler} is fit on the training partition only and applied to validation and test.
Formally, the scaler computes mean $\mu_j$ and standard deviation $\sigma_j$ for each feature $j$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}$:
\begin{align}
\mu_j &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}|} \sum_{(x_i, y_i) \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}} x_{i,j}, \\
\sigma_j &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}|} \sum_{(x_i, y_i) \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}} (x_{i,j} - \mu_j)^2},
\end{align}
and transforms all partitions: $\tilde{x}_{i,j} = (x_{i,j} - \mu_j) / \sigma_j$.
This train-only protocol prevents test-set statistics from leaking into the model and is the default for all WSN-DS experiments.

\subsection{Dual Evaluation Units}
\label{sec:dual-identity}
Fig.~\ref{fig:dual} defines two evaluation protocols on the same fixed stratified split:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multi-seed protocol (\ums{}):} Ten independent seeds with full route comparison (scratch, MLP-KD, RF-KD, co-distillation, curriculum). Each seed trains all routes before moving to the next.
\item \textbf{Deploy protocol (\udc{}):} Single seed-42 RF-KD only, used for ONNX export, fixed-point conversion, and two-board HIL.
\end{itemize}
The distinction is critical: seed-$42$ multi-seed RF-KD macro-F1 $0.9249$ is \emph{not} the deploy protocol seed-$42$ macro-F1 $0.9485$.

\subsection{Export, Fixed-Point, and HIL Protocol}
\label{sec:export}
deploy FP32 graphs are exported for host ONNX Runtime and OpenVINO evaluation.
MCU export uses post-training fixed-point quantization.
Given FP32 weights $w \in \mathbb{R}$ and activations $a \in \mathbb{R}$, we map to fixed-point:
\begin{align}
w_{\mathrm{fix}} &= \mathrm{round}(w / s_w), \\
a_{\mathrm{fix}} &= \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\mathrm{round}(a / s_a), -2^{n-1}, 2^{n-1}-1\right),
\end{align}
where $s_w, s_a > 0$ are scale factors determined by calibration, and $n=8$ for Q8.
The scale factor is computed from calibration data $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$:
\begin{equation}
s = \frac{\max_{x \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}} |f(x)|}{2^{n-1} - 1},
\end{equation}
ensuring the dynamic range covers observed activation magnitudes.
We disclose a float-to-fixed macro-F1 drop bound of $0.03$; a stricter $0.01$ bound is not claimed for these weights.

Quantization-aware training probes reduced deploy FP32 macro-F1 to $0.8996$ (Student~A, $\Delta = -0.018$) and $0.9213$ (Student~B, $\Delta = -0.023$), worse than post-training quantization at $0.9244$/$0.9180$; these QAT weights were not used for the reported HIL binaries.

Before MCU flashing, the fixed-point C kernel is cross-compiled for an MSP430F1611-class target (TelosB/Tmote Sky-style WSN mote, 48\,KB Flash, 10\,KB RAM) to verify memory feasibility on genuinely constrained hardware.
Under \texttt{-Os} with \texttt{msp430-elf-gcc} 9.3.1, the linked smoke firmware (integer StandardScaler preprocessing plus fixed-point MLP inference) requires $2{,}842$\,B Flash-class \texttt{.text}, $0$\,B \texttt{.data}, $6$\,B \texttt{.bss}, and a compiler-reported bounded project-function stack usage of approximately $248$\,B during prediction.
This cross-compile result supports MSP430/TelosB-class memory feasibility of the model core; it does not constitute physical deployment or measure real-time latency and energy on a live mote.

For each student--board pair, the host streams all $56{,}200$ test vectors; the MCU returns predictions; agreement is scored against the generated fixed-point reference.
Reported boards: ESP32-C3 and Arduino UNO~R4 WiFi.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} summarizes the end-to-end process.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{\method{} training and dual-MCU HIL workflow}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE \dataset{} features, fixed seed-$42$ split, student capacity $\{A,B\}$, seed set $\mathcal{S}$
\ENSURE Multi-seed tables (\ums{}), deploy artifacts (\udc{}), HIL agreement matrix
\STATE Fit \texttt{StandardScaler} on training partition only; transform val/test
\FOR{each seed $s \in \mathcal{S}$ under multi-seed protocol}
  \STATE Train RF teacher; calibrate soft targets; train routes (scratch, MLP-KD, RF-KD, J, curriculum, controls)
  \STATE Record accuracy, macro-F1, per-class F1
\ENDFOR
\STATE Aggregate \ums{} means and sample standard deviations; compute seed-paired KD$-$scratch tests
\STATE deploy unit: \texttt{set\_seed($42$)}; train RF-KD only for A and B
\STATE Export FP32; evaluate ONNX/OpenVINO; convert to fixed-point C under $0.03$ macro-F1 drop bound
\FOR{each board $\in \{$ESP32-C3, Arduino R4$\}$ and student $\in \{A,B\}$}
  \STATE Flash firmware; stream $N = 56{,}200$ test vectors over USB serial
  \STATE Score MCU predictions vs fixed-point reference; record latency
\ENDFOR
\STATE On \udc{}, compute global SHAP rank Spearman (RF TreeExplainer vs student DeepExplainer)
\STATE Secondary: Edge-IIoTset group-aware splits and leakage audit (no merge with \dataset{} scores)
\RETURN tables, figures, HIL matrix, SHAP diagnostics
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}

Table~\ref{tab:protocols} summarizes evaluation contracts.
Ten seeds for primary \dataset{} multi-seed work:
\[
\mathcal{S} = \{42, 123, 456, 789, 1001, 2024, 3141, 5678, 8192, 9999\}.
\]
Feature-group disjoint sensitivity uses five seeds $\{42, 123, 456, 789, 1001\}$ and $56{,}301$ test rows.
Edge-IIoTset appears only as a secondary robustness study with its own feature construction and $15$-class label space.

Primary metric is macro-F1 (equal class weight), motivated by the severe class imbalance in \dataset{} (Normal: $91\%$ of rows).
Macro-F1 is defined as the harmonic mean of per-class precision and recall, averaged across classes:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{macro\text{-}F1} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^{C} \frac{2 \cdot \mathrm{Prec}_c \cdot \mathrm{Rec}_c}{\mathrm{Prec}_c + \mathrm{Rec}_c},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Prec}_c$ and $\mathrm{Rec}_c$ are precision and recall for class $c$.
Accuracy is reported for continuity with prior work.
Per-class F1 exposes minority costs that macro-F1 can hide.
Multi-seed summaries use mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation.

For seed-paired comparisons, we use the paired $t$-test:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{\bar{\Delta}}{s_{\Delta} / \sqrt{n}}, \quad \bar{\Delta} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\mathrm{F1}_{\mathrm{KD}}^{(i)} - \mathrm{F1}_{\mathrm{scratch}}^{(i)}),
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the number of seeds and $s_{\Delta}$ is the sample standard deviation of the paired differences.
We also report two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank tests as a non-parametric alternative.
Given shared splits and modest $n$, $p$-values are interpreted as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

\subsection{SHAP Rank Transfer}
\label{sec:results-shap}
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) assigns each feature an importance value based on Shapley values from cooperative game theory~\cite{lundberg2017shap}.
For a model $f$ and input $x$, the SHAP value $\phi_j(f, x)$ for feature $j$ is:
\begin{equation}
\phi_j(f, x) = \sum_{S \subseteq [d] \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!\, (d - |S| - 1)!}{d!} \bigl[ f(x_{S \cup \{j\}}) - f(x_S) \bigr],
\end{equation}
where $x_S$ denotes the input with features not in $S$ replaced by background values and $[d] = \{1, \ldots, d\}$.
For tree models, TreeExplainer computes exact Shapley values in polynomial time.
For neural networks, DeepExplainer approximates Shapley values using backpropagation.

We measure global rank agreement using Spearman correlation on mean absolute SHAP values:
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d} (R_j^{\mathrm{RF}} - \bar{R}^{\mathrm{RF}})(R_j^{\mathrm{student}} - \bar{R}^{\mathrm{student}})}{\sqrt{\sum_{j} (R_j^{\mathrm{RF}} - \bar{R}^{\mathrm{RF}})^2} \sqrt{\sum_{j} (R_j^{\mathrm{student}} - \bar{R}^{\mathrm{student}})^2}},
\end{equation}
where $R_j$ is the rank of feature $j$ by mean $|\phi_j|$ across the test set.

Fig.~\ref{fig:shap} and Table~\ref{tab:shap-top} summarize the explanation-rank diagnostic.
Global mean-absolute SHAP rank Spearman correlation between RF TreeExplainer and student DeepExplainer on \udc{} is $\rho = 0.238$ ($p = 0.358$) for Student~A and $\rho = 0.225$ ($p = 0.384$) for Student~B.
Bootstrap means over five repeats are $0.178 \pm 0.086$ (A) and $0.162 \pm 0.055$ (B).
Top features diverge: Student~A global top features include \texttt{Is\_CH}, \texttt{JOIN\_S}, \texttt{dist\_CH\_To\_BS}; the RF teacher top features include \texttt{Is\_CH}, \texttt{ADV\_S}, \texttt{Data\_Sent\_To\_BS}, \texttt{DATA\_S}, \texttt{Rank}.
Shared presence of \texttt{Is\_CH} does not imply global rank agreement.
We treat this as a measured failure of global rank transfer, consistent with explanation disagreement literature~\cite{krishna2022disagreement}, not as evidence that RF-KD preserves RF explanations.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Evidence Supports}
Ultra-small RF-KD students retain competitive macro-F1 under train-only multi-seed evaluation at $4.64$\,KB and $13.27$\,KB FP32 payloads, achieving $58.8\times$ and $20.6\times$ compression relative to the neural teacher while preserving detection performance.
Dual-board full-test integer agreement is achievable for the deploy RF-KD unit, with float-to-fixed macro-F1 cost $0.024$--$0.027$ inside a disclosed $0.03$ bound and inference latencies from $116.5$\,$\mu$s to $791.4$\,$\mu$s.
Host FP32 conversion preserves predictions at agreement $1.0$.
These results support KB-scale executable neural detectors when evaluation units and protocol controls are reported carefully.

\subsection{What Requires Qualification}
KD gain vanishes under two independent corrections: feature-group splits ($\Delta = +0.0002$, $p = 0.95$) and per-route RNG isolation ($\Delta = +0.0007$ for A, $+0.0005$ for B). Either correction alone would be noteworthy; both converging on near-zero strengthens the conclusion that the multi-seed random-row KD advantage is protocol-contingent. KD benefit can also hide minority-class costs (Blackhole/Grayhole losses under feature-group).
Deploy seed-$42$ macro-F1 ($0.9485$ for Student~A) is not a multi-seed table cell ($0.9203 \pm 0.0065$ under \ums{}).
Global SHAP ranks are not preserved ($\rho \approx 0.24$, $p > 0.35$).
Co-distillation (J) underperforms RF-KD ($\Delta = -0.0046$ for A, $-0.0069$ for B).
Curriculum-extension can collapse under multi-route training (seed-$5678$ $\rightarrow$ $0.41$ macro-F1) even when isolated re-seeding recovers ($\approx 0.91$).

\subsection{Relation to Closest Prior Systems}
Javed \emph{et al.} demonstrate tree IDS on ESP32 for a different dataset and task, achieving $98.71\%$ binary accuracy with $276$\,$\mu$s inference~\cite{javed2024thermostat}.
Wisanwanichthan and Thammawichai demonstrate KD compression in software on workstation hardware~\cite{wisanwanichthan2025kd}.
Diab \emph{et al.} demonstrate hardware-aware models on Pi-class gateways with $95.3\%$ accuracy at $75$\,KB flash and $1.2$\,K operations for LightGBM~\cite{diab2025hardware}.
\method{} sits between these lines: RF-distilled neural students, WSN routing-attack labels, protocol ladder, two evaluation units, two-MCU HIL, and an explicit offline-feature evaluation boundary.
We do not claim first MCU IDS, first KD for IDS, or gateway-class deployment.

\subsection{Limitations}
Instrumented WSN motes and live radio energy measurements were unavailable; commercial MCUs proxy the constrained on-node class via offline feature replay.
Shared fixed splits limit claims of independence across seeds.
SHAP uses finite background and explain sample sizes and focuses on global ranks rather than local explanations.
HIL latencies are board means under serial replay, not worst-case real-time radio stacks.
Edge-IIoTset results use a different feature space and label set than WSN-DS and are not merged.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\method{} provides a protocol-honest account of RF-to-tiny neural distillation for WSN-DS intrusion detection.
Under multi-seed evaluation, Students~A and~B retain strong macro-F1 at $4.64$\,KB and $13.27$\,KB FP32 payloads ($58.8\times$ and $20.6\times$ compression), but KD benefit is protocol-contingent: significant under random-row ($p = 0.048$ for A) but vanishing under both feature-group splits ($\Delta = +0.0002$, $p = 0.95$) and per-route RNG isolation ($\Delta = +0.0007$ for A, $+0.0005$ for B)---two independent corrections that converge on the same conclusion.
A deploy protocol supports MSP430F1611-class cross-compile in $2{,}842$\,B Flash and dual-MCU full-test integer agreement ($1.0$ on all four student--board pairs) with measured float-to-fixed utility cost $0.024$--$0.027$ macro-F1.
Global SHAP ranks do not transfer reliably ($\rho \approx 0.24$, $p > 0.35$).
Co-distillation does not dominate RF-KD, and curriculum-extension instability under multi-route training must be disclosed.
These findings support KB-scale executable detectors when evaluation protocols, splits, and negative results are reported with the same rigor as compression ratios.

Future work includes instrumented radio motes with live mesh traffic and energy accounting, on-device feature extraction from packet streams, broader multi-dataset evaluation, and tighter quantization-aware training under the same two-protocol reporting.
